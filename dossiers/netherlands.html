Compiled for Domestic Data Streamers workshop use. Research current to July 2, 2026.

Legal accuracy rule applied throughout: only (a) FINAL criminal convictions (no further appeal possible, Dutch court and year stated), or (b) resignations following extensively documented scandals/institutional inquiries, are presented as fact about an individual. Nobody merely accused, indicted, on trial, or acquitted is named as a wrongdoer. The parliamentary inquiry into the childcare benefits scandal ("Ongekend Onrecht") is described as an institutional/political finding, not as an allegation of individual criminal guilt.

1. Country Snapshot

  • CPI 2025 (Transparency International): Netherlands scored 78/100, ranked 8th of 182 countries (published 4 February 2026), unchanged from 2024. Top scorers: Denmark (89), Finland (88), Singapore (84). Transparency.org CPI 2025
  • CPI 2024: 78/100, ranked 8th of 180, down from 79 in 2023. Transparency.org
  • CPI 2023: 79/100 — at the time the Netherlands' lowest-ever score, still top-10 globally (Norway 84, Singapore 83, Sweden/Switzerland 82, Netherlands 79). The score has gradually eroded from the low-to-mid 80s a decade ago. Transparency.org CPI 2023
  • Eurobarometer (Special Eurobarometer 523, fielded 2022): Dutch public opinion was evenly split — 50% believe corruption is widespread nationally, 50% say it is rare — a striking result for a top-10 "cleanest" country and one of the few EU states without a clear skew toward "rare." A precise 2025 Dutch-specific percentage could not be pin-cited from secondary sources reviewed (EU-wide, 69% of Europeans believe corruption is widespread, per the July 2025 Eurobarometer). Special Eurobarometer 523; Euronews, July 2025
  • GRECO Fifth Evaluation Round (published 22 February 2019): examined corruption prevention for top executive functions (ministers, state secretaries, advisors) and law enforcement. Issued 16 recommendations, including a code of conduct for top officials, lobbying and post-employment ("revolving door") rules, mandatory conflict-of-interest disclosure, and regular asset/interest declarations. GRECO Fifth Round Report
  • GRECO First Compliance Report (adopted 25 March 2021): found none of the 16 recommendations satisfactorily implemented — 8 "not implemented," 8 "partly implemented." GRECO First Compliance Report
  • GRECO Second Compliance Report (adopted 9 June 2023): noted progress but persistent gaps — the Netherlands still has no lobbying transparency register, relying instead on published ministerial agendas; a bill introducing a two-year post-ministerial lobbying ban was noted as pending. GRECO Second Compliance Report
  • GRECO Addendum (adopted 19 March 2025, published 27 November 2025): confirms continued incremental progress on the lobbying/post-employment bill, but full satisfaction of all 16 original recommendations had still not been reached. GRECO Addendum 2025

Analytical note: the Netherlands ranks 8th cleanest globally by CPI, yet half its own population does not believe that, and GRECO has spent over five years finding the country still lacks basic tools — a lobbying register, mandatory ad hoc conflict-of-interest disclosure, regular ministerial asset declarations — that most GRECO-compliant states already have.

2. Major Cases

2.1 Toeslagenaffaire (childcare benefits scandal) — institutional scandal, cabinet resignation

What happened: Between 2005 and 2019, the Dutch Tax and Customs Administration (Belastingdienst/Toeslagen) wrongly accused approximately 26,000 parents of fraudulently claiming childcare benefits, demanding full repayment — often tens of thousands of euros — under an all-or-nothing "hardness clause" policy allowing no room for minor administrative errors. Wikipedia

Discriminatory mechanism: the Tax Administration's fraud-detection unit applied an "80-20" presumption of fraud and factored dual nationality into its risk-selection models. In the worst-documented single case, "CAF 11 Hawaii," 317 clients of one childcare agency, almost all with dual citizenship, were classified as fraudsters in April 2014. The Dutch Data Protection Authority (Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens) concluded in July 2020 that the Tax Administration's methods were "unlawful, discriminatory and improper," finding a serious GDPR violation, while noting it could not legally characterize the practice as "ethnic profiling" in the strict GDPR sense since the service held nationality data, not race/ethnicity data. [Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens, 17 July 2020, cited in Wikipedia]

Parliamentary inquiry — "Ongekend Onrecht" ("Unprecedented Injustice"): the Parliamentary Committee of Inquiry into Childcare Benefits presented its final report to the House Speaker on 17 December 2020, criticizing the Tax Administration, the Ministry of Social Affairs, the Cabinet, the Council of State, and the House of Representatives itself, concluding the all-or-nothing approach violated "fundamental principles of the rule of law," and criticizing the so-called "Rutte doctrine" — a practice, named after a leaked civil-servant text message to PM Rutte, of withholding officials' internal policy deliberations from parliament and the public under the Government Information (Public Access) Act. Official "Ongekend Onrecht" report, Tweede Kamer, 17 December 2020; NOS

Cabinet resignation: following a no-confidence motion, the entire Third Rutte Cabinet resigned collectively on 15 January 2021, continuing only in caretaker capacity ahead of the March 2021 election. Minister of Economic Affairs and Climate Eric Wiebes resigned immediately and completely (not staying on as caretaker), as former state secretary for finance overseeing the Tax Administration. State Secretary for Finance Menno Snel had already resigned on 18 December 2019, after calling the Tax Administration's conduct "scandalous" in a House debate. Rijksoverheid.nl transcript, 15 January 2021; D66 statement on Snel

Compensation and total figures: on 22 December 2020, a flat €30,000 compensation payment was announced for every wrongly accused parent regardless of actual loss. As of 2024 reporting, 40,829 parents had been formally recognized as victims (gedupeerden) entitled to compensation and debt relief. The total cost of the compensation/restoration operation reached an estimated €11.6 billion by 2024/2025 reporting — more than double the €4.3 billion in childcare allowances originally recovered from all Dutch claimants between 2005–2020. De Correspondent; Algemene Rekenkamer, May 2024; Herstel Toeslagen

Children placed in out-of-home care: CBS (Statistics Netherlands) initially reported (October 2021) that 1,115 children of affected parents had been placed in out-of-home care since 2015; this was revised upward in April 2022 to 1,675 children placed between 2015 and end of 2021, of whom 555 remained outside the home at that time. A further Nederlands Jeugdinstituut report cites a cumulative 2,090 out-of-home placements, with 645 children still placed outside the home. Dutch investigative journalism (Follow the Money) has cautioned against a simplified "state kidnappings" narrative, since not every placement was solely and directly caused by the benefits clawback, but the scandal is documented as a significant contributing factor for the affected cohort. NOS; Nederlands Jeugdinstituut; Follow the Money

Criminal-justice outcome for the original fraud allegations: of the parents originally labeled fraudsters, only 200 were referred to the Public Prosecution Service for suspected forged documents, yielding just 15 convictions and 8 settlements — a strikingly small number against the 26,000 parents wrongly investigated. [Follow the Money, 24 December 2020, cited in Wikipedia]

Legal status note: no individual official or minister has faced a final criminal conviction over the Toeslagenaffaire. The consequences were exclusively political (resignations) and institutional/regulatory (the parliamentary inquiry and the Data Protection Authority's finding) — consistent with the strict rule, this is presented as a resignation/institutional-inquiry case, not a criminal-conviction case.

2.2 Jos van Rey (Roermond alderman; former Senator, VVD, later independent) — final conviction

Role: longtime Roermond alderman (1979–1982, 1998–2012), member of the States of Limburg, member of the House of Representatives (1982–1989, 1991–1998) and Senate (2011–2012), originally for the VVD. Resigned as alderman and senator in October 2012 when the corruption investigation began; expelled from the VVD in 2013.

Conduct: Van Rey was found to have accepted gifts, trips, services and promises — quantified in the original charge sheet around €97,699, with the Public Prosecution Service separately seeking recovery of €300,000 in corruptly obtained benefits — from real-estate developer Van Pol Beheer BV, in exchange for favoring the company in the award of major Roermond building/harbor development projects. He also leaked confidential mayoral-selection committee information in September 2012.

First instance: Rechtbank Rotterdam, 12 July 2016 — convicted on two counts of bribery, disclosure of confidential information, and electoral fraud; sentenced to the maximum 240 hours of community service, far below the two-year prison term prosecutors sought. NOS

Final conviction on appeal: Gerechtshof Den Haag (The Hague Court of Appeal), 20 December 2017 — found guilty of bribery, disclosure of confidential information, electoral fraud, and (added on appeal) money laundering; sentenced to a twelve-month suspended prison term plus disqualification from public office for two years. No further Supreme Court (Hoge Raad) cassation appeal overturning this ruling was found — the 2017 appellate conviction stands as final. Trouw, 20 December 2017

Aftermath: after his two-year disqualification, Van Rey returned to Roermond politics; his Liberal People's Party Roermond (LVR) has since become the largest party in Roermond in successive municipal elections.

2.3 Ton Hooijmaijers (former Deputy, Province of North Holland, VVD) — final conviction

Role: member of the Provincial Executive of North Holland responsible for Spatial Planning and Finance, 2005–2009.

Conduct: following an anonymous tip, the Fiscal Intelligence and Investigation Service (FIOD-ECD) and police raided Hooijmaijers' home, his mother's house, and six companies on 31 March 2010 on suspicion of bribery and corruption. The court ultimately found 11 separate instances of bribery and 13 instances of document forgery, and that he had laundered approximately €276,000. At trial he stated: "Corruptie en criminaliteit zijn mij vreemd" ("Corruption and criminality are foreign to me").

First instance: Rechtbank Haarlem, 3 December 2013 — sentenced to 3 years' imprisonment for bribery, money laundering and document forgery. BijzonderStrafrecht.nl

Appeal: Gerechtshof Amsterdam reduced the sentence to 2.5 years.

Final conviction: the Hoge Raad (Dutch Supreme Court) largely rejected his cassation appeal on 14 February 2017, leaving a final custodial sentence of 2 years and 4 months for bribery, forgery, and money laundering. This stands as one of the clearest final, definitive criminal corruption convictions of a Dutch provincial-level politician. NOS

2.4 Bouwfraude (construction fraud) parliamentary inquiry, 2002

Origin: on 9 November 2001, investigative program Zembla broadcast "Sjoemelen met miljoenen" ("Cheating with millions"), based on documents from whistleblower Ad Bos, former technical director of construction firm Koop Tjuchem, who supplied a shadow bookkeeping ledger documenting illegal price agreements across the construction sector from 1988 to 1998. Dutch Wikipedia, Ad Bos

Parliamentary inquiry: the House of Representatives established the Parliamentary Committee of Inquiry into the Construction Industry (chaired by Marijke Vos) on 5 February 2002; public hearings ran 22 August–27 September 2002. Its final report, "De bouw uit de schaduw" ("Construction out of the shadows"), concluded there was large-scale, systemic price-fixing and market-division fraud, but found no evidence of bribery of government officials — a narrower finding than "corruption" in the colloquial sense. The average illegitimate overcharge on public construction contracts was found to be 8.8%. Parlement.com

Euro figures: estimates vary by source and methodology. Whistleblower Ad Bos originally estimated the state was overpaying more than 100 million guilders annually; parliamentary sources put total potential claims at €120–150 million; broader Zembla/BNNVARA reporting describes roughly €15–25 billion in estimated illegal profits across the sector over the relevant decade (representing total distorted market value, not government overpayment specifically); the state ultimately recovered/benefited from an estimated €1 billion through fines, settlements, and recovered overcharges combined. BNNVARA/Zembla

Adjudicated administrative penalties: in October 2004, the Dutch competition authority (NMa) fined 345 construction companies a combined just over €100 million for cartel participation in the civil-engineering/road and waterworks sector, 1998–2001. In October 2005, the NMa fined 155 installation companies a combined €40 million for a parallel cartel. Combined confirmed NMa fines: approximately €140 million. ACM/NMa

Aftermath: the scandal led to strengthened NMa enforcement powers and broader Dutch competition-law reform; the NMa was later merged into the Authority for Consumers and Markets (ACM) in 2013. No individual final criminal corruption convictions of government officials resulted, consistent with the inquiry's own finding of fraud without proven official bribery. Whistleblower Ad Bos lost his job, income and home despite exposing the fraud, sued the Ministry of the Interior for €10 million in 2009, and ultimately received €1.7 million in damages.

2.5 Note on the scarcity of final convictions

Consistent with the pattern documented in academic and Transparency International Nederland commentary, final, definitive criminal corruption convictions of Dutch politicians are genuinely rare. Van Rey and Hooijmaijers are the two clearest, best-documented cases of definitive final convictions identified in this research; no additional cases of comparable seniority met the sourcing threshold for inclusion here.

5. Language of Denial

  1. Ton Hooijmaijers, at the close of his 2013 trial, while prosecutors sought a 4-year sentence: "Corruptie en criminaliteit zijn mij vreemd." ("Corruption and criminality are foreign to me.") He was convicted regardless — this is a documented denial-at-trial statement, not evidence of innocence. RTL Nieuws

  2. Mark Rutte, press conference, 15 January 2021, on his personal responsibility: "Ik ben geen bewindspersoon op Financiën of Sociale Zaken geweest in de afgelopen jaren, dus in die zin is die indirect, maar ik loop niet weg voor mijn verantwoordelijkheid." ("I have not personally been the minister for Finance or Social Affairs in recent years, so in that sense it [my responsibility] is indirect, but I am not running away from my responsibility.") Rijksoverheid.nl official transcript

  3. Mark Rutte, same press conference, responding to a suggestion the resignation was merely symbolic: "Nee. Het kabinet treedt af, neemt daarmee de volledige verantwoordelijkheid voor wat er zo vreselijk is misgegaan..." ("No. The cabinet is resigning, thereby taking full responsibility for what went so terribly wrong...") — followed under further press pressure by the more distanced: "Het is heftig wat er gebeurd is" ("What happened is intense/severe"), language critics noted stopped short of a personal admission of fault. [Rijksoverheid.nl transcript]

  4. Parliamentary Inquiry Committee, "Ongekend Onrecht" report, 17 December 2020, on institutional stonewalling: the committee found the Tax Administration had systematically failed to properly inform "de eigen bewindspersonen en de Tweede Kamer, als richting gedupeerde ouders, de rechterlijke macht en de media" (its own ministers, parliament, affected parents, the judiciary, and the media), enabled in part by the "Rutte doctrine" of shielding internal deliberations from scrutiny. Official "Ongekend Onrecht" report

  5. Ad Bos, bouwfraude whistleblower, on the institutional response to his disclosures: "De donkere kant van de overheid is doorgegaan met procederen tegen ons." ("The dark side of the government kept litigating against us.") BNNVARA

Key Caveats for This Dossier

  • The exact 2025 Eurobarometer Dutch-specific corruption-perception percentage could not be pin-cited from secondary sources; the most recent verified Dutch-specific figure is the 2022 50/50 split.
  • Out-of-home-placement figures for the Toeslagenaffaire come from CBS/Dutch government and NGO tracking and were revised upward twice (1,115 → 1,675 → 2,090) as more victims came forward; cite the specific report/date alongside any figure used.
  • No free Wikimedia Commons image was located for either Jos van Rey or Ton Hooijmaijers after multiple targeted searches.
  • The 2002 Bouwfraude inquiry explicitly found systemic fraud/cartel behavior but no proven official corruption/bribery of civil servants — this distinction should not be collapsed in any headline or caption reusing this material.
