Compiled 2 July 2026 for Domestic Data Streamers. Factual, sourced, non-editorial. Every named individual is described strictly per their documented legal status as of this date. Method note: this dossier applies one hard rule throughout — a person is only described as "convicted" if a court verdict or guilty plea has produced a final sentence with appeals exhausted or waived. Being charged, arraigned, indicted, under investigation, or having resigned is described as exactly that, and never blurred into "convicted." Where Malta has more resigned/charged officials than convicted ones, that gap is reported honestly rather than smoothed over.

Estimated cost of corruption

No independently audited, Malta-specific cost estimate exists. The figure most often cited in Maltese media — €725 million per year (roughly 8.65% of GDP) — originates from a December 2018 report commissioned by the Greens/EFA group in the European Parliament, a political advocacy body, not an independent statistical agency. Source. It should be treated as an advocacy-derived estimate, not a verified figure.

GRECO (Group of States against Corruption, Council of Europe)

  • Fourth Evaluation Round (MPs, judges, prosecutors) compliance report, adopted 2 December 2022, published 6 June 2023: of 9 recommendations, 4 satisfactorily implemented, 4 partly, 1 not implemented. On MPs specifically: "it is very disappointing that more than seven years after the issuing of the recommendations, none of them have been fully complied with." Source
  • Fifth Evaluation Round (executive/law enforcement, including the Attorney General) second compliance report, adopted 1 December 2023, published 21 March 2024: of 23 recommendations, only 4 satisfactorily implemented, 10 partly, 9 not implemented. GRECO concluded Malta was "not in sufficient compliance." Source
  • Latest addendum, adopted 6 June 2025, published 28 November 2025: 8 satisfactory, 7 partly, 8 not implemented. On lobbying rules: "This situation is clearly unsatisfactory." On investigative techniques: "The continued inaction of the authorities over the years is clearly unsatisfactory." This report triggered an escalation letter from the Council of Europe's Secretary General. Source

MONEYVAL / FATF grey list

  • Malta was placed on the FATF list of "Jurisdictions under Increased Monitoring" (the grey list) on 25 June 2021 — the first EU member state ever grey-listed. Source
  • Malta was removed on 17 June 2022, after roughly 12 months, following an action plan addressing deficiencies in prosecuting complex money-laundering and financial-crime cases. Source; Malta Business Registry
  • MONEYVAL's Fifth Round Mutual Evaluation (adopted 18 July 2019, published 12 September 2019) had found: "law enforcement authorities are currently not in a position to effectively and in a timely manner pursue high-level and complex money laundering cases related to financial, bribery and corruption offences." Source

2. Major Cases

Case 1: The Assassination of Daphne Caruana Galizia

Years: 2017 (murder) – 2026 (trial of alleged mastermind ongoing) Institution involved: State security/policing apparatus; Office of the Prime Minister (per public inquiry findings)

Mechanism: On 16 October 2017, investigative journalist Daphne Caruana Galizia — who had reported extensively on the Panama Papers, Egrant allegations, and Malta's offshore economy — was killed by a car bomb near her home in Bidnija. The case involves distinct groups of people with distinct, non-interchangeable legal outcomes.

Convicted (final): - Alfred Degiorgio ("il-Fulu") and George Degiorgio ("iċ-Ċiniż"), brothers who procured, planted, and detonated the bomb: pleaded guilty to wilful homicide and criminal conspiracy. Sentenced 14 October 2022 to 40 years in prison each plus a €50,000 fine each. Appeal dismissed by the Court of Appeal on 21–22 November 2023. Final. ICIJ; Malta Independent - Vincent "Vince" Muscat ("il-Koħħu"): pleaded guilty at a pretrial hearing in February 2021 as part of a plea bargain, in exchange for information on the bomb's suppliers. Sentenced to 15 years in prison; explicitly renounced his right to appeal. Final. (Separately, he received a presidential pardon for an unrelated 2015 murder, in exchange for testimony in that case — not for the Caruana Galizia killing.) CPJ - Robert Agius and Jamie Vella, who supplied the explosives: convicted by jury verdict (8–1) of complicity in murder, participation in a criminal organisation, illicit possession of explosives, and complicity in causing an explosion intended to kill. Sentenced to life imprisonment, 10 June 2025. Appeal (alleging juror misconduct and prejudicial publicity) dismissed 21 January 2026. Final. Daphne Caruana Galizia Foundation; CPJ; OCCRP

Not a defendant — pardoned in exchange for testimony: - Melvin Theuma, the confessed middleman who wore a wire for police and named those he said were responsible, received a presidential pardon in 2019 (recommended by then-PM Joseph Muscat) covering his role and unrelated past offences, conditional on truthful cooperation. He was never prosecuted for the murder. The full pardon text has never been published; as of January 2025 the President's Office was still declining to release it in full. The Shift News

Awaiting trial — not convicted: - Yorgen Fenech, businessman and owner of the Tumas Group (linked to the offshore company "17 Black"), was indicted in 2021 on charges of complicity in wilful homicide and criminal association, and identified by prosecutors as the alleged mastermind. He was held in detention from 2019, released on bail in February 2025, and his jury trial opened on 1 July 2026 before Madam Justice Edwina Grima — he pleaded not guilty. The trial is expected to run roughly eight weeks. As of this writing (2 July 2026) Fenech has not been convicted of anything related to the murder; the case is at the start of trial. The Attorney General is seeking a life sentence if convicted. In a separate, unrelated case (an alleged fraudulent public-sector job for Melvin Theuma), Fenech was acquitted in November 2025 — this acquittal has no bearing on the murder trial. The Shift News; ICIJ; RSF

Web of connections: Fenech is separately a defendant (not convicted) in the "17 Black"/Electrogas money-laundering case alongside former chief of staff Keith Schembri and former minister Konrad Mizzi (see Case 2). No police officer has been identified in public reporting as finally convicted of corruption or complicity tied to this case.

Aftermath: A public inquiry (437 pages, delivered 29 July 2021) concluded that the Maltese state bears responsibility for the assassination, though it found no evidence the state directly ordered or participated in the killing. Its central finding: the state created "an atmosphere of impunity" that "spread to regulatory bodies and the police," comparable to "an octopus," such that "anyone who wanted to eliminate her could do so with the least possible consequences." The Muscat-era cabinet was found "collectively responsible" for inaction on threats against her. Malta Independent; ICIJ

Case 2: Panama Papers — Konrad Mizzi and Keith Schembri

Years: 2013 (companies opened) – 2016 (leak) – 2019 (resignations) – 2021–2025 (subsequent charges) Institution involved: Office of the Prime Minister; Ministry for Energy and Health

Mechanism: In April 2016, the Panama Papers leak revealed that Konrad Mizzi (then a minister) and Keith Schembri (chief of staff to PM Joseph Muscat) had each opened secret Panama companies — Mizzi's Hearnville Incorporated (9 July 2013) and Schembri's Tillgate Inc. (15 July 2013) — within months of the Labour government taking office in March 2013, using Maltese firm Nexia BT and Panama-based Mossack Fonseca. A third company in the same batch, Egrant Inc., was alleged by Daphne Caruana Galizia to be secretly owned by Michelle Muscat, the Prime Minister's wife. A leaked December 2015 email later identified 17 Black, a Dubai company owned by Yorgen Fenech, as the intended "target client" designed to pay Hearnville and Tillgate up to €150,000 per month each — allegedly tied to the Electrogas power-station deal that Mizzi, as Energy Minister, had helped structure.

Resignations: - Konrad Mizzi resigned from Cabinet on 26 November 2019, amid the political crisis following Yorgen Fenech's arrest in the murder investigation. He was expelled from the Labour Party's parliamentary group by a 71–1 vote on 23 June 2020 (proximate trigger: a separate wind-farm scandal, layered atop the Panama Papers fallout), remaining an independent MP until March 2022. Times of Malta; Times of Malta - Keith Schembri resigned as chief of staff on 26 November 2019, in the same political crisis, after being named by Fenech to police. Times of Malta; BBC

Subsequently charged — not convicted: Neither man has a final corruption conviction. Both have been charged in multiple, separate, still-unresolved cases: - Schembri was first charged on 20 March 2021 with corruption, fraud, and money laundering over an alleged bribery scheme with a printing-contract executive; granted bail 5 April 2021. The Shift News - Both men were charged on 28 May 2024, alongside former PM Joseph Muscat and 19 others, in the Vitals/Steward hospitals case (see Case 3) — corruption, bribery, money laundering, fraud, criminal association. Pleaded not guilty; asset-freezing orders of up to €30 million each. Indicted (prima facie finding) on 25 June 2024 — an indictment, not a conviction. OCCRP - Both men were arraigned again in early February 2025, with Yorgen Fenech and others, in the "17 Black"/Electrogas money-laundering case. Not guilty pleas; asset freezes (€12 million for Mizzi). The Critical Angle Project - Schembri was additionally charged on 8 November 2024 with perjury, false oaths, obstruction, and breach of the Official Secrets Act, over allegations he leaked confidential murder-investigation material to Fenech. Not guilty plea. Times of Malta - On 22 December 2021, the US State Department publicly designated both men for "involvement in significant corruption" — an administrative/diplomatic sanction, not a criminal charge or conviction. US Department of State

As of the most recent reporting located (November 2025), all of the above cases remain at pre-trial or trial stages. No source found describes either man as finally convicted of any charge.

Egrant inquiry (related, separate matter): A 15-month magisterial inquiry, concluded around 21–22 July 2018, found no evidence that Egrant belonged to Joseph or Michelle Muscat and no evidence of the alleged $1.07 million payment reaching them — while separately finding that a document purporting to link Egrant to Michelle Muscat contained falsified signatures. The Caruana Galizia family disputed the inquiry's exculpatory framing, noting the magistrate could not say Muscat was "guiltless," only that no ownership evidence was found. Malta Independent

Aftermath: The Panama Papers resignations were the first major crack in the Muscat government and set the template — offshore structuring exposed by leak, denial, gradual political cost — repeated in every subsequent Malta scandal below.

Case 3: Joseph Muscat's Resignation and the Vitals/Steward Hospitals Concession

Years: 2015 (concession signed) – 2019/2020 (resignation) – 2023 (contract annulled) – 2024 onward (criminal charges) Institution involved: Office of the Prime Minister; Ministry of Health

Muscat's resignation: Joseph Muscat announced his resignation as Prime Minister on 1 December 2019, leaving office on 13 January 2020. The precise, documented reason was not a corruption conviction: it followed sustained public protests after the 20 November 2019 arrest of Yorgen Fenech in the Caruana Galizia murder investigation, and the resignation/brief arrest of his own chief of staff, Keith Schembri, whom Fenech had named to police. Muscat was never personally charged in the murder case; he resigned over the political fallout and collapse of public confidence in his government's handling of the investigation. Wikipedia, cross-checked against contemporary reporting; OCCRP

The hospitals mechanism: In 2015, the Muscat government signed a 30-year concession handing over three public hospitals — St Luke's, Karin Grech (both Malta), and Gozo General Hospital — to Vitals Global Healthcare (VGH), a newly formed company with no track record in hospital management. Reporting has cited payments to VGH/Steward of roughly €188,000 per day (approximately €70 million per year) for hospital beds, on top of fees tied to a Barts Medical School franchise arrangement and a helicopter service, while promised capital investment went largely undelivered. In February 2018, VGH transferred the concession to Steward Health Care International for a nominal €1. Cumulative payments through 2023 have been estimated at roughly €400 million, against a headline 30-year projected contract value cited in different sources as between €1.3 billion and €2.1 billion (estimates vary by methodology). Euronews

2023 civil annulment (not a criminal conviction): On 24 February 2023, Mr Justice Franco Depasquale ruled the concession agreement null and void, finding fraud had been committed both before and after the contract was awarded, and when Steward assumed VGH's obligations. The Court of Appeal upheld the annulment on 23 October 2023, describing collusion between VGH/Steward and government officials. The hospitals reverted to public management. This was a civil/commercial ruling, legally distinct from the separate criminal case below — no individual was criminally convicted through this ruling. The Shift News; The Critical Angle Project

Criminal charges — charged, not convicted: Following a four-year magisterial inquiry (a 1,200-page report), Joseph Muscat was arraigned on 28 May 2024 alongside Keith Schembri, Konrad Mizzi, and 19 other individuals and companies (22 defendants total), including Steward Malta's legal representative David-Joseph Meli. Charges include money laundering, fraud, criminal conspiracy, bribery/corruption of public officials, and organizing a criminal organization. All defendants pleaded not guilty; asset-freezing orders of up to €30 million each were imposed on Muscat, Schembri, and Mizzi. On 25 June 2024, a magistrate issued a prima facie decree finding sufficient evidence for the case to proceed toward indictment — the case remains at the pre-trial "compilation of evidence" stage as of late 2025, with no trial date confirmed and procedural friction reported (including a forensic expert's refusal to testify in March 2025, and Schembri's own October 2025 filing alleging misconduct by court-appointed experts). OCCRP; MaltaToday

No one has been criminally convicted in this case as of this writing. Muscat's status is: resigned (Jan 2020, for reasons unrelated to a corruption finding) → separately charged (May 2024) → indicted (June 2024) → case ongoing, unresolved.

Aftermath: The scandal fed directly into Malta's FATF grey-listing (see Section 1) and into GRECO's repeated findings of non-compliance on prosecutorial independence.

Case 4: Pilatus Bank

Years: 2014 (licensed) – 2018 (license revoked) – 2019–2020 (US criminal case, then vacated) Institution involved: Pilatus Bank plc (private bank); European Central Bank; Malta Financial Services Authority (MFSA); Financial Intelligence Analysis Unit (FIAU)

Mechanism: Pilatus Bank, founded by Iranian-American businessman Ali Sadr Hasheminejad, was licensed in Malta in 2014 and became central to Daphne Caruana Galizia's Egrant reporting, which alleged the bank processed payments linked to politically exposed persons, including an alleged Azerbaijani-linked transaction to Egrant. The Egrant inquiry (see Case 2) did not substantiate the alleged link to Michelle Muscat, but separate regulatory findings on the bank's own conduct proceeded independently.

Regulatory action (not a criminal conviction): The European Central Bank revoked Pilatus Bank's banking license on 4 November 2018, following an MFSA withdrawal recommendation. The Guardian; OCCRP. Malta's FIAU separately fined Pilatus Bank a then-record €4.9 million in August 2021 for anti-money-laundering failures — an administrative penalty, not a criminal sentence. Times of Malta

Ali Sadr Hasheminejad's US case — convicted, then vacated (does not meet the conviction threshold): Sadr was indicted in the US in March 2018 on charges of violating Iran sanctions through a $115 million scheme routing Venezuelan construction payments through the US financial system. A jury in the Southern District of New York convicted him on five counts on 16 March 2020. However, after prosecutors failed to disclose exculpatory evidence to the defense in a timely manner, they moved to drop their own case in June 2020, and on 17 July 2020 Judge Alison J. Nathan vacated the guilty verdict and dismissed the indictment with prejudice — meaning the US Department of Justice is permanently barred from retrying him. A subsequent opinion formally reprimanded the prosecutors for misconduct. Sadr has no standing criminal conviction anywhere; his case does not meet this dossier's conviction threshold and he is not listed as convicted. NPR; SDNY opinion

Other individuals: Claude-Anne Sant Fournier, Pilatus Bank's former head of legal/compliance and money-laundering reporting officer, was charged with money laundering in Malta and pleaded not guilty; no final verdict was found in reporting. No individual connected to Pilatus Bank has a final corruption conviction anywhere, in Malta or elsewhere. MaltaToday

Aftermath: The bank's collapse and license revocation became a central example cited in Malta's MONEYVAL and FATF grey-list assessments of weak enforcement against high-level financial crime.

Case 5: The "17 Black" / Electrogas Case

Years: 2013 (Electrogas deal structured) – 2015 (leaked email identifying 17 Black) – 2025 (criminal arraignment) Institution involved: Ministry for Energy; Electrogas Malta consortium (power station project)

Mechanism: A leaked email from Nexia BT's Karl Cini to Mossack Fonseca, dated 17 December 2015, named 17 Black — a Dubai company owned by Yorgen Fenech — as the "target client" intended to pay up to €150,000 per month each into Konrad Mizzi's and Keith Schembri's Panama companies (Hearnville and Tillgate respectively). The arrangement is alleged to be connected to the Electrogas power-station contract, which Mizzi's ministry helped structure, and in which Fenech's Tumas Group held a stake. A magisterial inquiry into this arrangement concluded in late 2024, recommending charges. MaltaToday

Charged — not convicted: In early February 2025, Yorgen Fenech, Keith Schembri, Konrad Mizzi, Karl Cini, Brian Tonna, Paul Apap Bologna, and Mario Pullicino were arraigned on charges including money laundering, corruption, trading in influence, and criminal association (Mizzi individually also charged with perjury). All pleaded not guilty; a court found sufficient prima facie evidence on 27 February 2025 to send the case toward trial. Asset freezes were imposed (€12 million on Mizzi). This case remains pending as of mid-2026; no convictions have occurred. The Critical Angle Project; Malta Daily

Aftermath: This case formally links the Panama Papers offshore structures, the Caruana Galizia murder investigation (via Fenech), and Malta's energy-sector procurement in a single prosecution — illustrating how the separate scandals in this dossier are not isolated incidents but overlapping strands of the same alleged network.

Note on the gap between resignations and convictions: Of the individuals named above who held political office (Mizzi, Schembri, Muscat), none has a final corruption conviction as of 2 July 2026, despite all three having resigned amid scandal and all three now facing active, unresolved criminal charges. This gap — extensively documented, not a gap in reporting — reflects the pace of Malta's judicial process, repeatedly criticized by GRECO and MONEYVAL (Section 1), rather than a lack of prosecutorial activity.

5. Language of Denial

  1. On the Panama Papers, Prime Minister Joseph Muscat, 6 April 2016: "I want the people to understand that we get it. I am not happy with the Panama Papers situation…" — while describing Keith Schembri, whose undeclared millions had just been revealed, merely as "an employee of the State." MaltaToday

  2. On his own Panama company, Minister Konrad Mizzi, 2016: "I did what I did in the best interest of my family, but I recognize this issue has cast a shadow on myself and on the government. There is nothing illicit in what I did, but I know I have political responsibility." Lovin Malta

  3. On the Egrant inquiry findings, the Muscat government's official statement, 23 July 2018, describing the original allegation as "the greatest lie in Malta's political history." MaltaToday

  4. On why Daphne Caruana Galizia was killed, former PM Joseph Muscat, reported 13 July 2022, recalling his own state of mind before her murder: "[My] government had survived her most virulent onslaught so for me personally it was almost a time of sitting back, taking the popcorn out and enjoying the show... And then she gets killed, which didn't make any sense to me." Newsbook

  5. On the magisterial inquiry into the hospitals concession, which ultimately led to his own indictment, Prime Minister Robert Abela, 28–29 April 2024: "Justice does not mean political terrorism." MaltaToday
