Compiled for a Domestic Data Streamers workshop. Sources are cited inline. Individuals are named only where they meet a strict legal-inclusion rule (see note below). This is a research compilation for educational/journalistic purposes, comparable to Transparency International or OCCRP country profiles.

Inclusion rule applied throughout: a person is named as "convicted" only if they hold a final ("pravomocný") criminal conviction for a corruption-related offense, or is named as "resigned" only if they resigned from office over an extensively documented scandal — labelled explicitly as "resigned, not convicted." People who are only accused, indicted, under active trial, or acquitted (including cases where an acquittal is itself still under appeal) are described by role/institution only, with the legal status stated explicitly. Where a case involves EU institutional findings on conflicts of interest rather than a criminal charge, this dossier attributes those findings explicitly to the institution (the European Commission, the European Court of Auditors) rather than framing them as an allegation of individual criminal conduct.

1. Country snapshot

  • Transparency International Corruption Perceptions Index (CPI) 2024 (published 11 Feb 2025): Czechia scored 56/100, ranking 46th of 180 countries — down 1 point and 5 places versus 2023, and six points below the EU average. Transparency.org — Czechia 2024 | Brno Daily, 11 Feb 2025
  • CPI 2023: Czechia scored 57/100. Transparency.org — Czechia 2023 | Radio Prague International
  • Eurobarometer / Global Corruption Barometer: a Czechia-specific figure from the most recent Special Eurobarometer corruption survey could not be confirmed with confidence for this dossier (the country-level breakdowns are distributed as dashboard/PDF annexes rather than through directly citable web pages); the EU-wide figure for the same survey wave is 68–69% of citizens considering corruption widespread nationally. A precisely sourced, comparable figure specific to Czechia is Transparency International's Global Corruption Barometer 2022: 85% of Czechs said government corruption is a big problem, and 57% said they had used personal connections to access public services — the highest rate recorded in the EU survey. European Commission Eurobarometer | eucrim, citing the Global Corruption Barometer
  • EU funds and conflict of interest: unlike Hungary or Poland, Czechia has not faced a rule-of-law conditionality freeze of EU funds. The relevant EU institutional finding is narrower and specific: the European Commission's Directorates-General for Regional Policy, Employment, and Agriculture published a final audit report on 23 April 2021 finding that then-Prime Minister Andrej Babiš was in a conflict of interest under Article 61 of the EU Financial Regulation, through his beneficial ownership structure (via AB Private Trust I and II) over the Agrofert conglomerate, while it received EU cohesion and agricultural subsidies. A 2019 preliminary/leaked draft cited approximately €11 million (non-agricultural funds) and €17.4 million (agricultural subsidies) as sums potentially requiring return; however, Commissioner Johannes Hahn told the European Parliament on 19 May 2021 that "so far no expenditure has been declared for any of the operations affected by this audit," meaning, in the Commission's own words, that "the EU budget has been and remains fully protected." This is an institutional conflict-of-interest finding, not a criminal conviction. European Parliament press release, 17 May 2021 | France24, 1 Jun 2019
  • The European Court of Auditors' Special Report 06/2023 on conflict of interest references the Czech case as an example (without naming Babiš or Agrofert directly) of the structural risk that beneficial-ownership opacity poses to EU fund oversight across member states. ECA Special Report 06/2023
  • GRECO (Council of Europe): GRECO's Fifth Round Evaluation Report on Czechia (presented 4 March 2024) issued 20 recommendations on the integrity of top executive officials and law enforcement. A subsequent compliance assessment found only 1 of the 20 recommendations fully implemented, 6 partially implemented, and 13 not implemented at all. GRECO Fifth Round Evaluation Report (Council of Europe PDF) | Brno Daily on the compliance follow-up

2. Major cases and systems

2.1 David Rath — final convictions on two separate procurement-bribery indictments (2012–2025)

Institutions: Central Bohemian Regional Authority; hospital and public-building procurement (Buštěhrad Castle, Hostivice Gymnasium, Kladno/Kolín/Mladá Boleslav hospital pavilions, ambulance tenders).

The arrest: David Rath, former Czech Health Minister and later President of the Central Bohemian Region, was arrested in May 2012 with boxes of cash — famously including wine crates stuffed with banknotes — in what became the country's highest-profile corruption arrest of the decade.

Branch I (Buštěhrad Castle / Hostivice Gymnasium tenders): the Prague High Court delivered its verdict on 26 June 2019, convicting Rath and sentencing him to 7 years' imprisonment plus forfeiture of CZK 17 million. Co-defendants Kateřina Kottová and Petr Kott each received 6 years' imprisonment and CZK 15 million in forfeiture. The Constitutional Court rejected related complaints over wiretap evidence on 7 June 2019, and the Supreme Court rejected all further extraordinary appeals ("dovolání") around March 2021, closing this branch permanently. Radio Prague International | iROZHLAS, Supreme Court rejection, March 2021

Branch II (Kladno/Kolín/Mladá Boleslav hospital pavilions, ambulance tenders): the Prague High Court delivered a verdict on 21 June 2022 upholding conviction on this second indictment, with a combined sentence of 8 years' imprisonment, a CZK 13 million fine, and an 8-year ban from holding public office; Kottová and Kott received matching sentences to their roles in this branch. Construction company Metrostav was separately fined CZK 10 million with a 3-year public-procurement ban. The Supreme Court rejected all extraordinary appeals on this branch around 27 March 2025, confirming finality. iROZHLAS, 21 Jun 2022 | Aktuálně.cz/ČTK, March 2025

Legal status: convicted, final, on both indictments (finalized 2021 and 2025 respectively). Rath has since served his sentence.

2.2 Marek Dalík — final conviction for fraud in the Pandur armored-vehicle procurement case (2009–2019)

Institution: Ministry of Defence; the Pandur II armored personnel carrier procurement program with the Austrian/Steyr-linked consortium.

The case: lobbyist Marek Dalík, a close associate of former PM Mirek Topolánek, was accused of brokering an arrangement around the Czech military's Pandur II procurement in which he obtained payment from the supplier by claiming influence over government ministers. An earlier Prague Municipal Court conviction was annulled by the Supreme Court on legal-classification grounds and the case was retried.

Final verdict: the Prague High Court delivered its verdict at the end of July 2017, sentencing Dalík to 5 years' imprisonment, but on a reclassified charge of attempted fraud, not bribery — the court found that Dalík had deceived the supplier's representative by falsely claiming influence over ministers, rather than having actually brokered a bribe to a government official. His second Supreme Court appeal ("dovolání") was rejected around 22 February–7 March 2018, and a subsequent Constitutional Court complaint was also rejected. He began serving his sentence on 6 November 2017 and was conditionally released around 18 November 2019, having served half the term. e15.cz | info.cz | Radio Prague International

Legal status: convicted, final (2017–2018) — for attempted fraud, not bribery. This precise legal characterization is preserved deliberately: Dalík's final conviction was for deceiving a private company, not for corrupting a public official, even though the case is widely discussed in the context of defense-procurement corruption.

2.3 Andrej Babiš — Čapí hnízdo ("Stork's Nest") case: unresolved, no final verdict either way

Institutions: Agrofert; a EU small/medium-enterprise subsidy program; the Chamber of Deputies (parliamentary immunity).

Status: this case remains legally unresolved and must not be described as either a conviction or a final acquittal. Prosecutors alleged that the Čapí hnízdo farm and conference complex was artificially separated from the Agrofert conglomerate to qualify for a roughly CZK 50 million (~€2 million) EU small/medium-enterprise subsidy in 2007–2008 that it would not otherwise have qualified for while under Agrofert's ownership.

Timeline: - Municipal Court in Prague acquitted Babiš and co-defendant Jana Nagyová (then his chief of staff) on 9 January 2023; Babiš declared: "INNOCENT!" The prosecution appealed. - The Prague High Court annulled that acquittal in September 2023, sending the case back for retrial. - The Municipal Court again acquitted both defendants on 14 February 2024. - The Prague High Court annulled the second acquittal as well, on 23 June 2025, again returning the case for further proceedings and instructing the lower court, as a binding legal opinion, that Babiš and Nagyová knew Čapí hnízdo did not meet the small/medium-enterprise definition and that the separation from Agrofert was intentional — findings the court said could constitute the criminal offenses of subsidy fraud and damaging the EU's financial interests. Note: this is an appellate court's instruction on legal reasoning for the retrial, not a verdict of guilt. - The case was then split: in a verdict reported in May 2026, the Prague Municipal Court, sitting for the first time as first instance on an individual defendant's guilt in this case, found Jana Nagyová guilty, handing her a 3-year suspended sentence and a CZK 500,000 fine — a first-instance verdict that remains appealable and is not final. - Babiš's own portion of the case was excluded from that proceeding, because the Chamber of Deputies voted 104–81 against lifting his parliamentary immunity on 6 March 2026 (he had returned to office as Prime Minister in December 2025), meaning criminal proceedings against him personally could not continue at that time.

Euronews (via English-language reporting), 9 Jan 2023 acquittal | Radio Prague International, 2023 acquittal | Brno Daily, 23 Jun 2025 annulment | Brno Daily, 6 Mar 2026 immunity vote | iROZHLAS, May 2026 Nagyová verdict

Legal status: unresolved. Babiš has neither a final conviction nor a final acquittal; per the strict inclusion rule he is not named in this dossier's People Index as either "convicted" or "acquitted." Co-defendant Jana Nagyová's May 2026 conviction is first-instance only and not yet final.

Separate and distinct — the EU Commission's institutional conflict-of-interest finding: independent of the criminal case above, the European Commission's 23 April 2021 final audit (see Section 1) found that Babiš held a conflict of interest under EU financial rules through his Agrofert ownership structure while serving as Prime Minister and approving EU funding decisions. This is an administrative/institutional finding by the European Commission concerning conflict of interest, explicitly not a criminal finding, and the Commission itself noted that no EU expenditure was ultimately paid out on the affected operations. Agrofert has publicly disputed the audit's findings and figures. European Parliament press release | Agrofert's own statement on the audit

2.4 Ivo Rittig — no final conviction in any traced case; final acquittals throughout

Czech lobbyist Ivo Rittig, linked in press reporting to Prague's public transport company (DPP) and several other procurement controversies, was investigated in multiple cases, all of which concluded in final acquittal:

  • DPP ticketing case (Neograph/Cross Point/Crowsnest, alleged CZK 457 million in damage): final acquittal by the Prague High Court in September 2021, with no further appeal filed; Rittig was subsequently awarded CZK 990,000 in compensation for wrongful prosecution. ČT24, Sept 2021
  • Oleo Chemical case (alleged tax evasion and money laundering): charges against Rittig were dropped at trial in February 2017; the Prague High Court rejected the prosecutor's appeal, making the acquittal final in June 2019. Echo24, Jun 2019
  • BIS leak case (disclosure of a classified intelligence report): Rittig and co-defendants were acquitted in November 2019; the Prague High Court dismissed the prosecutor's appeal, making the acquittal final on 1 October 2020. Deník, Oct 2020
  • Opencard scandal: Rittig was named in press reporting as an alleged beneficiary of irregularities in Prague's Opencard transit-card system, but no evidence of a formal personal prosecution of Rittig in that specific matter was found.

Legal status: no final conviction exists for Ivo Rittig in any traced case. Per the strict inclusion rule, he is excluded from this dossier's "convicted" category and is described here only via his documented final acquittals, for context on how a high-profile corruption investigation can conclude.

2.5 The Stoka Brno case — final conviction via approved plea agreement (2015–2025)

Institution: Brno-střed district municipal authority (town hall), public-procurement and IT-services contracts.

The network: Czech investigators uncovered a corruption network at the Brno-střed district town hall, alleged to have operated between 2015 and 2019 under an internal hierarchy that steered public contracts and non-public tender information to favored companies in exchange for bribes. The network was led by Jiří Švachula, a former deputy mayor of Brno-střed (at the time affiliated with the ANO party), using the internal code name "George."

First verdict (later annulled): the Regional Court in Brno convicted 9 of 11 defendants on 31 May 2022 — Švachula received 9.5 years' imprisonment; co-defendants El-Talabani and Ovčarčin received 6 years each; others received suspended sentences; two defendants were acquitted. This verdict was annulled in its entirety by the High Court in Olomouc on 28 November 2024, which cited serious procedural flaws and ordered a retrial. Brno Daily, 31 May 2022 | Brno Daily, 28 Nov 2024

Final verdict, via plea agreement: the Regional Court in Brno approved plea agreements in the retrial in early December 2025. Švachula's plea agreement set his sentence at 7 years and 8 months' imprisonment (reduced from the annulled 9.5-year term) plus partial forfeiture of assets; co-defendant Smolka received a suspended sentence of 2 years 10 months, a CZK 3 million fine, and a 10-year ban from public office; co-defendant Ovčarčin received 5 years' imprisonment and a CZK 600,000 fine. Czech court-approved plea agreements ("dohoda o vině a trestu") take legal effect immediately upon court approval and are not subject to ordinary appeal, meaning this December 2025 verdict is final. Brno Daily, 5 Dec 2025 | iROZHLAS, Dec 2025

Legal status: convicted, final (December 2025, via approved plea agreement) — Jiří Švachula, 7 years 8 months' imprisonment; co-defendants Smolka and Ovčarčin also finally convicted with the sentences described above.

Related, distinct, and unresolved: a separate Brno-střed municipal-flats ("byty") case, involving ODS politician Otakar Bradáč, remains under investigation and is not addressed further here as it has produced no final verdict.

6. Methodology and workshop-use notes

On sourcing: every factual claim above carries an inline citation, drawn primarily from Radio Prague International, iROZHLAS, Brno Daily, and Czech national press (e15.cz, info.cz, Deník, ČT24) reporting on court proceedings, cross-checked against European Commission and European Parliament documents for the Babiš/Agrofert conflict-of-interest material. Where finality of a verdict could not be independently confirmed against a primary court record — the December 2025 Stoka Brno plea-agreement sentences, for instance — this is flagged explicitly rather than asserted.

On the Babiš case specifically: Čapí hnízdo is the dossier's clearest illustration of why the strict finality rule matters. Public discussion of this case has, at various points since 2023, described Babiš as "acquitted," implying the matter is settled; in fact, both acquittals to date (January 2023 and February 2024) were annulled on appeal by the Prague High Court, most recently in June 2025, and the case remains open, only stalled because parliament declined to lift his immunity while he serves as Prime Minister. This dossier treats that status as what it is — unresolved — rather than defaulting to either "acquitted" or "under suspicion of guilt." The distinct European Commission conflict-of-interest finding is reported entirely separately, as an institutional administrative finding rather than a criminal matter, in line with the strict inclusion rule's instruction on systemic EU-funds material.

On the fraud/bribery distinction in the Dalík case: Marek Dalík's final conviction (Section 2.2) is legally for attempted fraud against a private company, not bribery of a public official — even though the underlying scandal is popularly remembered as a bribery case tied to a major defense-procurement contract. This dossier preserves that legal nuance deliberately, since collapsing it into a generic "corruption conviction" would overstate what the courts actually found.

On Ivo Rittig as a comparison case: Rittig is included in this dossier specifically because his cases illustrate the opposite pattern from Rath's or Švachula's — a lobbyist widely named in Czech investigative journalism across at least three major procurement and leak scandals, none of which produced a surviving conviction. Facilitators may find this a useful pairing with the Rath and Stoka Brno cases to discuss why some Czech corruption investigations end in final convictions and others do not, despite similar levels of public and media attention.
