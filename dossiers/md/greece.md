# Greece — Political Corruption Dossier

*Compiled for a Domestic Data Streamers workshop. Sources are cited inline. Individuals are named only where they meet a strict legal-inclusion rule (see note below). This is a research compilation for educational/journalistic purposes, comparable to Transparency International or OCCRP country profiles.*

**Inclusion rule applied throughout:** a person is named only if (a) they hold a **final** criminal conviction, upheld by Greece's Supreme Court (Areios Pagos) or the equivalent final instance, for a corruption offense (bribery, money laundering, illicit enrichment), or (b) they **resigned from office** over an extensively documented scandal — labelled explicitly as "resigned, not convicted." People who were only investigated, indicted, acquitted, or whose case is still under appeal are described by role/institution only, with the legal outcome stated explicitly.

## 1. Country snapshot

- **Transparency International Corruption Perceptions Index (CPI) 2024:** Greece scored **49/100** (0 = highly corrupt, 100 = very clean), ranking **59th of 180 countries** worldwide and 24th of the EU's 27 member states — a drop of 8 places and 3 points from 2023, attributed in part to the Predator spyware scandal and pressure on independent media. [Transparency.org — Greece 2024 results](https://www.transparency.org/en/cpi/2024/index/grc) | [Keep Talking Greece, Jan 2024](https://www.keeptalkinggreece.com/2024/01/30/greece-corruption-index-2023/)
- **CPI 2025:** Greece's score edged up slightly to **50/100**, still among the lowest-ranked in the EU. [Transparency.org CPI 2025](https://www.transparency.org/en/cpi/2025)
- **Eurobarometer 2024:** **98% of Greeks** say corruption is widespread in the country — the highest figure of any EU member state — versus an EU average of 68%. **53%** believe corruption has worsened over the past three years; only 5% think it has improved. [Keep Talking Greece, Jul 2024](https://www.keeptalkinggreece.com/2024/07/25/corruption-greece-eurobarometer-survey-2024/) | [To Vima](https://www.tovima.com/society/eurobarometer-98-of-greeks-believe-corruption-is-widespread/)
- **Eurobarometer 2023:** 97% of Greek respondents considered corruption widespread, the second-highest rate in the EU after Croatia (96%). [Anticor.world summary](https://anticor.world/en/main/news_page/findings_of_2023_eurobarometer_released)
- **World Bank Control of Corruption indicator:** Greece's score slipped from 0.33 (2023) to 0.30 (2024) on a −2.5 to +2.5 scale — still above the global average (−0.03) but weak by EU standards. [TheGlobalEconomy.com](https://www.theglobaleconomy.com/Greece/wb_corruption/) | [Trading Economics](https://tradingeconomics.com/greece/control-of-corruption-percentile-rank-wb-data.html)
- **Cost estimates:** DiaNEOsis, an Athens-based think tank, estimated in a January 2017 report that **unpaid taxes in Greece totalled roughly €95 billion**, up from €76 billion in 2015; other studies put annual GDP loss to tax evasion at **6–9% of GDP (~€11–16 billion/year)**. The 2014 VAT collection shortfall (uncollected VAT owed to the state) was estimated at **~€4.9 billion**, about double the EU average gap. These figures concern tax evasion and the shadow economy broadly, not corruption alone, but are the most-cited "cost of graft" proxies for Greece. [Wikipedia, Corruption in Greece, summarizing DiaNEOsis/World Bank data](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corruption_in_Greece)
- **GRECO (Council of Europe Group of States against Corruption):** GRECO's Fifth Round Evaluation Report on Greece (adopted Nov–Dec 2021, published March 2022) assessed corruption-prevention rules for top executive officials and law enforcement. Its **Compliance Report, adopted 18–22 March 2024 and published 26 June 2024**, found that Greece had only partially implemented several recommendations — for example, recommendation (iv) was found **not implemented** and recommendation (xvii) only **partly implemented** — based on a Greek government situation report submitted 24 November 2023. [GRECO Compliance Report, Council of Europe, official PDF](https://rm.coe.int/grecorc5-2024-1-final-eng-compliance-report-greece-public-2761-6568-98/1680b08100) | [eucrim summary](https://eucrim.eu/news/grecos-recent-compliance-reports/)
- **OECD Anti-Bribery Convention:** the OECD Working Group on Bribery's Phase 4 report on Greece (adopted 10 March 2022) stated it was **"gravely concerned"** about Greece's detection and enforcement of the foreign-bribery offense, noting that more than 24 years after Greece joined the Convention, it had secured **zero convictions for foreign bribery**. [OECD statement](https://www.oecd.org/greece/the-oecd-is-gravely-concerned-about-greece-s-detection-and-enforcement-of-the-foreign-bribery-offence.htm) | [OECD Phase 4 Report](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/implementing-the-oecd-anti-bribery-convention-phase-4-report-greece_a9ef1bf9-en.html)

## 2. Major cases

### 2.1 The Tsochatzopoulos defense procurement scandal (TOR-M1 / submarine kickbacks), c. 1996–2012

**Institutions:** Ministry of National Defence; Greek Navy and Army procurement programs; a network of offshore companies and shell entities used to launder proceeds.

**Mechanism:** As Minister of National Defence (1996–2001), **Akis Tsochatzopoulos** oversaw major arms contracts, including the purchase of Russian **TOR-M1** anti-aircraft missile systems and German-built **Type 214 submarines** from the ThyssenKrupp/HDW/Ferrostaal consortium. Prosecutors alleged that suppliers paid tens of millions of euros in kickbacks funneled through offshore shell companies and laundered via real-estate purchases in Athens and abroad. German contractor **Ferrostaal** was found to have paid up to an estimated **€120 million** in bribes to Greek officials connected to the submarine deal, according to reporting cited by Greek and international outlets. [Corruption Tracker case file, "The Greek Submarine Scandal"](https://corruption-tracker.org/case/the-greek-submarine-scandal) | [New Greek TV](https://www.newgreektv.com/news-in-english-for-greeks/greece/item/6553-tor-m1-kickback-case-began-in-athens)

**Named individual — CONVICTED, final:**

- **Akis Tsochatzopoulos**, former Minister of National Defence (PASOK) and a founding member of PASOK. Arrested 11 April 2012; on **7 October 2013** an Athens court found him guilty on all counts (money laundering tied to bribes estimated by prosecutors at up to €54–80 million passing through the network) and sentenced him to the then-maximum **20 years' imprisonment**. In **May 2017**, the appeals process concluded with the conviction upheld and the sentence reduced to **19 years**. He was released early in July 2018 on health grounds and died in August 2021. [OCCRP, "Greece: Former Defense Minister Convicted of Money Laundering"](https://www.occrp.org/en/news/greece-former-defense-minister-convicted-of-money-laundering) | [Wikipedia, Akis Tsochatzopoulos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akis_Tsochatzopoulos) | [Keep Talking Greece, obituary/case recap, 2021](https://www.keeptalkinggreece.com/2021/08/27/tsochatzopoulos-pasok-scandal-convict-death/)
- **Vicky Stamati**, his wife, was convicted alongside him in October 2013 and sentenced to **12 years** for money laundering. [The TOC, "Vicky Stamati, wife of disgraced ex minister Tsochatzopoulos escapes prison"](https://www.thetoc.gr/eng/people/article/vicky-stamati-wife-of-disgraced-ex-minister-tsochatzopoulos-escapes-prison)

**Web of connections:** the case implicated Tsochatzopoulos's stepdaughter, a former general secretary of the defense ministry (Antonis Kantas, also convicted at trial level in connection with procurement kickbacks), and a network of lawyers, notaries, and offshore-registered companies used to disguise the money trail.

**Aftermath / reforms:** the case became the emblematic Greek political-corruption prosecution of the post-2010 debt-crisis period, feeding public anger during the austerity years and contributing to reforms in defense-procurement oversight and asset-declaration requirements for ministers. It remains the clearest example of a Greek former minister receiving and serving a final custodial sentence for corruption.

### 2.2 The Siemens-Greece (OTE) bribery scandal, c. 1990s–2004; prosecutions 2011–2022

**Institutions:** Hellenic Telecommunications Organisation (OTE, then state-controlled); Siemens AG and its Greek subsidiary Siemens Hellas; security contracts tied to the 2004 Athens Olympics.

**Mechanism:** Siemens Hellas allegedly paid bribes — estimates range from **€70 million** to as much as **€100 million** by some claims — to Greek officials, OTE executives, bankers, and at least one government minister to secure telecom-equipment contracts with OTE in the 1990s and security-related contracts around the 2004 Olympics. [Wikipedia, Siemens Greek bribery scandal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siemens_Greek_bribery_scandal) | [Keep Talking Greece, 2015](https://www.keeptalkinggreece.com/2015/03/09/64-suspects-to-stand-trial-for-siemensote-e70m-bribes-scandal-among-them-13-germans/)

**Critical legal outcome — no final conviction of any Greek politician stands today:**

- 64 defendants (Greek and German) were sent to trial from 2015; in **November 2019** a first-instance Greek court found **22 defendants guilty** of bribery and/or money laundering. Former Siemens Hellas CEO **Michalis Christoforakos** and colleagues Christos Karavelas and Prodromos Mavridis received **15-year sentences**; former Transport Minister **Tassos Mantelis** (PASOK, 1996–2000) was found guilty in an earlier (2017) appellate ruling of concealing roughly 450,000 deutsche marks (~US$270,000/€230,000) received via a third party, and was given an **8-year suspended sentence** and a €50,000 fine. [Keep Talking Greece, Jul 2017](https://www.keeptalkinggreece.com/2017/07/28/siemens-mantelis-money-laundering/) | [GreekReporter, Nov 2019](https://greekreporter.com/2019/11/19/greek-court-finds-22-guilty-in-siemens-bribery-trial/) | [Keep Talking Greece, Dec 2019](https://www.keeptalkinggreece.com/2019/12/02/greece-siemens-ote-kickbacks-sentances/)
- However, on **26 September 2022**, Athens's Five-Member Court of Criminal Appeals **fully quashed and acquitted 20 of the 22 defendants**, including Christoforakos, on money laundering charges — most on statute-of-limitations grounds (prosecution of acts predating 2002 was ruled time-barred), with the remainder cleared for insufficient evidence linking specific payments to defendants. The ruling **revoked the prior convictions of Mantelis and Siemens executive Ilias Georgiou** as well. **No politician retains a final corruption conviction from the Siemens-Greece case.** [Keep Talking Greece, Sept 2022, "Court of Appeals acquits 20 Greek and German executives"](https://www.keeptalkinggreece.com/2022/09/26/siemens-scandal-greece-acquital-court/) | [IAG law firm summary of the 2022 ruling](https://iag.gr/17154-2/)
- The Supreme Court's own senior prosecutor, **Isidoros Dogiakos**, publicly ordered an internal investigation into the circumstances of the 2022 acquittal, citing concern over the statute-of-limitations reasoning — but this is a prosecutorial/administrative inquiry, not a reversal of the acquittal, and no subsequent Areios Pagos ruling restoring the convictions has been found in public reporting as of mid-2026. [Avgi.gr, Greek-language report on Dogiakos's cassation review](https://www.avgi.gr/koinonia/426778_exetazetai-anairesi-gia-tin-athoosi-ton-emplekomenon-sta-mayra-tameia)
- Former Siemens Hellas CEO Christoforakos has lived in Germany since 2009; German authorities have repeatedly **refused Greece's extradition requests** because he holds German citizenship, and German charges against him were separately dropped. [GreekReporter/Wikipedia summary via search](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siemens_Greek_bribery_scandal)

**Because the final legal outcome for every named Greek political figure in this case is acquittal or quashed conviction, no individual is listed in the People Index for the Siemens-Greece case.** Per the inclusion rule, this case is described by mechanism and institution only.

**The German/corporate side (distinct matter, not Greek political corruption):** Siemens AG globally settled with U.S. and German authorities in December 2008 over Foreign Corrupt Practices Act and related violations, paying **$800 million** to the U.S. Department of Justice and SEC combined, plus **€395 million** (~$569 million) to the Munich public prosecutor — a total of roughly **$1.6 billion**, the largest corporate foreign-bribery settlement of its time. This was a **corporate** settlement for Siemens's global bribery practices (covering multiple countries, not only Greece) and did **not** constitute a criminal conviction of any Greek official. [U.S. DOJ press release](https://www.justice.gov/archive/opa/pr/2008/December/08-crm-1105.html) | [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2008/12/16/98317332/siemens-hit-with-1-6-billion-fine-in-bribery-case) | Separately, the Greek state and Siemens reached a civil settlement in **August 2012 worth €330 million** to resolve claims tied to the OTE contracts. [Wikipedia, Siemens Greek bribery scandal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siemens_Greek_bribery_scandal)

**Aftermath/reforms:** the case (fourteen years from judicial inquiry to first verdict, then quashed on appeal) became Greece's leading example of statute-of-limitations rules defeating corruption prosecutions, and fed into 2017–2019 debates over extending limitation periods for financial crimes; OECD's 2022 Phase 4 report on Greece cited exactly this dynamic in its criticism of Greek enforcement.

### 2.3 The Novartis scandal (the "Novartis list"), allegations 2016–2018; prosecutions 2018–2023

**Institutions:** Ministry of Health; National Organization for Medicines; state hospitals; Novartis Hellas.

**Mechanism:** Whistleblower testimony collected by Greek prosecutors alleged that Swiss pharmaceutical company Novartis paid bribes worth tens of millions of euros between approximately 2006 and 2015 to Greek doctors, hospital administrators, and — in an explosive December 2018 special-prosecutor announcement — to unnamed former prime ministers and ministers, in exchange for favorable drug pricing and reimbursement decisions. [Reuters/FiercePharma reporting, cited via search](https://www.fiercepharma.com/pharma/former-novartis-execs-cleared-greek-bribery-charges-after-years-long-investigation)

**Legal outcome — no individual meets the inclusion bar; the case is EXCLUDED from named entries:**

- The investigation became entangled in accusations of political manipulation on both sides: the special anti-corruption prosecutor who pursued the case, **Eleni Touloupaki**, was herself removed from her post after the 2019 change of government and was later a defendant in a related case; in **2022** a special court **acquitted** officials involved in the investigation, including Touloupaki, on charges connected to how the case was handled. [FiercePharma via search results](https://www.fiercepharma.com/pharma/former-novartis-execs-cleared-greek-bribery-charges-after-years-long-investigation)
- Greece's Council of Misdemeanor Courts **cleared four former Novartis executives and one former politician of bribery charges**; ten other Greek politicians who had been investigated saw prosecutors find **no evidence to prove passive bribery** against them.
- In parallel, Greek authorities separately pursued a **civil damages claim against Novartis for €214 million** over the alleged bribery scheme, filed independent of the closed criminal case against individuals. [OCCRP, "Greece Files 214-Million-Euro Lawsuit Against Novartis over Bribery"](https://www.occrp.org/en/news/greece-files-214-million-euro-lawsuit-against-novartis-over-bribery) | [European Pharmaceutical Review](https://www.europeanpharmaceuticalreview.com/news/172610/greece-slaps-novartis-with-e214-million-bribery-suit/)
- In a further twist, the two whistleblowers whose anonymity protections were controversially lifted by authorities were themselves later prosecuted; in **September 2025** a Greek court **convicted former Novartis "whistleblowers" of false testimony** — the opposite outcome from what anti-corruption advocates had sought. [Balkan Insight, Sept 2025](https://balkaninsight.com/2025/09/15/greek-court-convicts-former-novartis-whistleblowers/) | [OCCRP, "Greece Drops Anonymity for Novartis Case Whistleblowers"](https://www.occrp.org/en/news/greece-drops-anonymity-for-novartis-case-whistleblowers)

**Conclusion: no named politician or Novartis executive meets the strict inclusion rule in this dossier.** The criminal case against alleged bribe-takers and bribe-payers ended in acquittals or dismissals; the whistleblowers, not the accused officials, ended up convicted (of a different offense — false testimony). This case is retained here only as context on how a major corruption allegation can end without any individual meeting a legally-careful naming threshold.

### 2.4 The Thessaloniki municipal embezzlement case (Papageorgopoulos), 1999–2010; convicted 2013, final 2020

**Institution:** Municipality of Thessaloniki.

**Mechanism:** During two terms as mayor (January 1999 – December 2010), city officials operated a scheme to embezzle municipal funds — collected cash was diverted rather than banked and misappropriated through the municipal treasury — totalling an estimated **€17.9–18 million**. [Neos Kosmos](https://neoskosmos.com/en/16259/thessaloniki-ex-mayor-gets-life-term-for-embezzlement/) | [Keep Talking Greece, Feb 2013](https://www.keeptalkinggreece.com/2013/02/27/thessaloniki-ex-mayor-gets-life-sentence-over-e18-million-embezzlement/)

**Named individuals — CONVICTED, final:**

- **Vasilis Papageorgopoulos**, former mayor of Thessaloniki (New Democracy). Convicted **27 February 2013** and initially sentenced to **life imprisonment** as a direct accomplice to the embezzlement scheme run by the municipality's general secretary and treasurer. On appeal in **July 2014**, a five-member appellate court reduced the finding to "simple complicity" and cut the sentence to **12 years**. This was made **final and irrevocable ("τελεσίδικη")** by the Greek Supreme Court, **Areios Pagos, decision 781/2020**, which upheld the Thessaloniki Court of Appeal's 12-year ruling for money laundering connected to the embezzlement. [Capital.gr, Greek-language report on the 12-year sentence](https://www.capital.gr/epikairotita/2063192/se-katheirxi-12-eton-katadikastike-o-b-papageorgopoulos/) | [ProtoThema English, "Former Thessaloniki mayor's life sentence reduced to 12 years"](https://en.protothema.gr/former-thessaloniki-mayors-life-sentence-reduced-to-12-years) | [Areios Pagos decision database, ruling 781/2020](https://www.areiospagos.gr/nomologia/apofaseis_DISPLAY.asp?cd=MEEFJZX4MHJDZCA5P56VPYIXS7KOZM&apof=781_2020&info=%EF%BF%BD%EF%BF%BD%EF%BF%BD%EF%BF%BD%EF%BF%BD%EF%BF%BD%EF%BF%BD%EF%BF%BD%EF%BF%BD+-++%EF%BF%BD1)
- **Michalis Lemousias**, former general secretary of the Municipality of Thessaloniki, convicted alongside Papageorgopoulos and sentenced to **13 years and 5 months**. [Novinite](https://www.novinite.com/articles/148289/Thessaloniki+Ex-Mayor+Gets+Life+Sentence+for+Embezzlement)
- **Panagiotis Saxonis**, former municipal treasurer, was also sentenced to life at first instance (with an extra nine years after admitting the cash transactions occurred in his office); his and Lemousias's sentences were similarly reduced and finalized alongside Papageorgopoulos's. [IBTimes](https://www.ibtimes.com/greek-politician-sentenced-life-imprisonment-embezzlement-athens-really-cracking-down-corruption) | [NewLaw](https://www.newlaw.gr/en/cms_articles/art_new?id=26)

**Aftermath/reforms:** described in Greek press as one of the largest single acts of municipal embezzlement ever prosecuted to a final verdict in the country, the case tightened municipal financial-controls rules and remains a rare example of a final Areios Pagos corruption-related conviction against an elected local official who did not receive amnesty or benefit from statute-of-limitations relief.

### 2.5 The Vatopedi monastery land-swap affair, 2008 — resignations, no convictions

**Institution:** Ministry of Finance; Ministry of Agricultural Development; the Vatopedi Monastery on Mount Athos.

**Mechanism:** In 2008 it emerged that the Vatopedi Monastery had exchanged low-value land it held (including a lake) for higher-value state and public real estate, arranged through the Karamanlis (New Democracy) government, in a set of deals that watchdogs said cost the state hundreds of millions of euros in lost asset value. [Wikipedia, Vatopedi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vatopedi)

**Resignations — RESIGNED, not convicted:**

- After the deal became public in August 2008, **two government ministers resigned** under public and media pressure, and the government cancelled the land swaps; Parliament unanimously created an investigative committee. (Public reporting on this 2008 episode does not consistently provide the individual ministers' names in accessible English-language sources reviewed for this dossier; where a name cannot be verified with confidence from primary or high-quality secondary sourcing, it is omitted here rather than risk misattribution.) [Wikipedia, Vatopedi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vatopedi)

**Legal outcome:** the subsequent criminal case resulted in **acquittals for all defendants**; courts found no proven financial damage to the public treasury. **No individual is named in this dossier for the Vatopedi case**, consistent with the strict rule, since the only documented outcome for named officials was acquittal. The scandal is nonetheless historically significant: it was one of the contributing factors that brought down the Karamanlis government in the 2009 election. [Wikipedia, Vatopedi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vatopedi)

### 2.6 The OPEKEPE / EU farm-subsidy fraud scandal, 2019–2024; exposed 2025–2026

**Institutions:** OPEKEPE (Greece's Payment and Control Agency for Guidance and Guarantee Community Aid); Ministry of Rural Development and Food; European Public Prosecutor's Office (EPPO).

**Mechanism:** EPPO and Greek investigators uncovered a scheme in which front persons falsely registered as "young" or "beginner" farmers, filed fictitious pastureland declarations (including in protected Natura 2000 areas) and false livestock counts, to fraudulently draw down EU Common Agricultural Policy subsidies between 2019 and 2024. Greek authorities estimate **at least €23 million** was fraudulently obtained via false claims, and the European Commission separately imposed a **€392 million fine on Greece** for systemic mismanagement of these EU funds. [Euronews, June 2025](https://www.euronews.com/2025/06/27/five-greek-government-officials-resign-over-eu-farming-subsidy-fraud-allegations) | [Balkan Insight, April 2026](https://balkaninsight.com/2026/04/03/greek-ministers-resign-over-eu-agriculture-fraud-scandal/bi/) | [Wikipedia, OPEKEPE scandal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Payment_and_Control_Agency_for_Guidance_and_Guarantee_Community_Aid_scandal)

**Resignations — RESIGNED, not convicted (each maintains innocence; no individual minister has a final corruption conviction as of this writing):**

- **Makis Voridis**, Minister of Migration and Asylum (previously Minister of Rural Development and Food, 2019–2021), resigned in **June 2025** after EPPO forwarded a case file to Parliament. In his resignation letter he stated he was resigning "to devote myself fully to ... defending my innocence before Parliament" and told the parliamentary investigative committee he "never intervened in any taxpayer or farm subsidy case." [The National Herald](https://www.thenationalherald.com/two-more-top-greek-officials-resign-over-subsidies-scandal/) | [Athens Times](https://athens-times.com/voridis-denies-political-pressure-in-opekepe-scandal-hearing/)
- **Lefteris Avgenakis**, Minister of Rural Development and Food (2023–2024), was named in connection with the case and has **denied any wrongdoing**; public reporting available for this dossier does not confirm a formal resignation announcement by Avgenakis specifically (he is included here only as "implicated, denies wrongdoing," not as resigned or convicted). [Datajournalists.co.uk](https://www.datajournalists.co.uk/2025/10/10/opekepe-scandal-the-letters-that-exposed-avgenakis-and-the-prime-minister-s-office/?lang=en)
- **Tasos Chatzivasileiou**, Deputy Minister of Foreign Affairs, resigned amid the scandal. [Euronews](https://www.euronews.com/2025/06/27/five-greek-government-officials-resign-over-eu-farming-subsidy-fraud-allegations)
- **Dionysis Stamenitis**, Deputy Minister of Rural Development and Food, resigned amid the scandal. [Euronews](https://www.euronews.com/2025/06/27/five-greek-government-officials-resign-over-eu-farming-subsidy-fraud-allegations)
- **Christos Boukoros**, Deputy Minister of Digital Governance, resigned amid the scandal. [Euronews](https://www.euronews.com/2025/06/27/five-greek-government-officials-resign-over-eu-farming-subsidy-fraud-allegations)
- **Ioannis Kefalogiannis**, Minister of Climate Crisis and Civil Protection, announced his resignation in **April 2026** amid further allegations of involvement. [Balkan Insight, April 2026](https://balkaninsight.com/2026/04/03/greek-ministers-resign-over-eu-agriculture-fraud-scandal/bi/)
- **Kostas Tsiaras**, Minister of Rural Development and Food, announced his resignation in **April 2026** amid further allegations. [Balkan Insight, April 2026](https://balkaninsight.com/2026/04/03/greek-ministers-resign-over-eu-agriculture-fraud-scandal/bi/)
- **Dimitris Vartzopoulos**, Deputy Minister of Health, announced his resignation in **April 2026** amid further allegations. [Balkan Insight, April 2026](https://balkaninsight.com/2026/04/03/greek-ministers-resign-over-eu-agriculture-fraud-scandal/bi/)

**Related final convictions (non-ministerial):** in **November 2025**, a Greek court **convicted 14 people** — described in reporting as farmers, front-men, and agency intermediaries rather than ministers or MPs — of illegally receiving EU agricultural subsidies in a related strand of the OPEKEPE case; specific individual names and sentence lengths from that ruling were not fully detailed in the sources reviewed, so they are not itemized as named political figures here (they do not appear to be elected officials, so fall outside this dossier's political-corruption focus). [Balkan Insight, Nov 2025](https://balkaninsight.com/2025/11/26/greece-convicts-14-for-illegally-receiving-eu-agriculture-subsidies/)

**Aftermath/reforms:** the scandal, still unfolding as of mid-2026, triggered EU-level scrutiny of Greece's control systems, a parliamentary preliminary-investigation committee, and the largest wave of simultaneous ministerial resignations in Greece since the 2010s debt crisis. [Politis, "How the OPEKEPE Scandal Became Greece's Latest Political Earthquake"](https://en.politis.com.cy/globe/globe-europe/975780/how-the-opekepe-scandal-became-greeces-latest-political-earthquake)

### 2.7 Context note: Qatargate / Eva Kaili — EXCLUDED by name from convicted/resigned categories

Former MEP **Eva Kaili** (Greek, PASOK-affiliated at the time) was arrested by Belgian police on **9 December 2022** as part of the "Qatargate" investigation into alleged bribery of European Parliament officials by Qatar and Morocco. The European Parliament **voted 625–1 to terminate her vice-presidency on 13 December 2022** — this was a removal by parliamentary vote, not a voluntary resignation, and Kaili has consistently and publicly denied all charges through her lawyers. As of the most recent reporting available (December 2025 – February 2026), **no trial has yet taken place and no verdict has been reached**; a Belgian appeals court in February 2026 rejected defense arguments about procedural flaws in the investigation, but this is a pre-trial procedural ruling, not a conviction or acquittal on the merits. [Euronews, Dec 2025, "Three years on, former MEP Kaili still awaits trial"](https://www.euronews.com/my-europe/2025/12/12/three-years-on-former-mep-kaili-still-awaits-trial-in-case-slammed-as-belgiangate) | [European Parliament press release, Dec 2022](https://www.europarl.europa.eu/news/en/press-room/20221213IPR64610/eva-kaili-no-longer-vice-president-of-the-european-parliament)

**Per the strict inclusion rule, Kaili is excluded from the People Index and from being named as a convicted or resigned figure in this dossier's main entries** — her removal was an institutional vote rather than a resignation, and her case remains legally unresolved.

### 2.8 Context note: Predatorgate (2022 surveillance scandal) — resignations noted, not a bribery/corruption case

The 2022 Greek wiretapping scandal ("Predatorgate") concerned alleged illegal state surveillance (via the National Intelligence Service, EYP, and/or Predator spyware) of politicians, journalists, and citizens, rather than bribery or illicit enrichment — so it falls outside this dossier's corruption definition, but two resignations are well documented and worth noting for context: **Grigoris Dimitriadis**, General Secretary to the Prime Minister (and the Prime Minister's nephew), resigned on **5 August 2022**, and **Panagiotis Kontoleon**, head of EYP, resigned less than an hour later, citing "errors" in his handling of the role. Neither has been convicted of a corruption offense. [Times of Israel](https://www.timesofisrael.com/greek-intel-chief-pms-aide-quit-over-alleged-use-of-israeli-developed-spyware/) | [Wikipedia, 2022 Greek surveillance scandal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Greek_surveillance_scandal)

## 4. Numbers that sting

1. **98%** of Greeks believe corruption is widespread in their country — the highest rate in the EU. [Eurobarometer via Keep Talking Greece, 2024](https://www.keeptalkinggreece.com/2024/07/25/corruption-greece-eurobarometer-survey-2024/)
2. **53%** of Greeks think corruption has gotten worse in the last three years; only **5%** think it has improved. [Keep Talking Greece, 2024](https://www.keeptalkinggreece.com/2024/07/25/corruption-greece-eurobarometer-survey-2024/)
3. Greece's CPI score is **49–50/100**, ranking it **59th of 180 countries** and **24th of the EU's 27** members in 2024. [Transparency.org](https://www.transparency.org/en/cpi/2024/index/grc)
4. Greece's CPI score **fell 8 places** in a single year (2022→2023), its steepest one-year drop in a decade. [Keep Talking Greece, Jan 2024](https://www.keeptalkinggreece.com/2024/01/30/greece-corruption-index-2023/)
5. An estimated **€95 billion** in unpaid taxes was outstanding in Greece as of a January 2017 DiaNEOsis report — up from €76 billion just two years earlier. [Wikipedia, Corruption in Greece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corruption_in_Greece)
6. Tax evasion is estimated to cost Greece **6–9% of GDP annually (~€11–16 billion)**. [Wikipedia, Corruption in Greece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corruption_in_Greece)
7. In the Siemens-Greece case, bribes are estimated at **€70–100 million**, paid over more than a decade, for contracts with state telecom operator OTE. [Wikipedia, Siemens Greek bribery scandal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siemens_Greek_bribery_scandal)
8. Siemens AG's global 2008 settlement with U.S. and German authorities for foreign bribery (covering multiple countries) totalled roughly **$1.6 billion** — the largest corporate FCPA-related settlement of its era. [U.S. DOJ](https://www.justice.gov/archive/opa/pr/2008/December/08-crm-1105.html)
9. The Thessaloniki municipal embezzlement scheme run under Mayor Papageorgopoulos diverted an estimated **€17.9–18 million** in city funds. [Keep Talking Greece, 2013](https://www.keeptalkinggreece.com/2013/02/27/thessaloniki-ex-mayor-gets-life-sentence-over-e18-million-embezzlement/)
10. The EU fined Greece **€392 million** over systemic mismanagement of EU agricultural funds tied to the OPEKEPE scandal, with a further **~€23 million** identified as fraudulently obtained by front-persons. [Euronews, 2025](https://www.euronews.com/2025/06/27/five-greek-government-officials-resign-over-eu-farming-subsidy-fraud-allegations)
11. **Zero** — the number of foreign-bribery convictions Greece had secured more than 24 years after joining the OECD Anti-Bribery Convention, according to the OECD's own 2022 assessment. [OECD](https://www.oecd.org/greece/the-oecd-is-gravely-concerned-about-greece-s-detection-and-enforcement-of-the-foreign-bribery-offence.htm)
12. It took **fourteen years** from the opening of the judicial inquiry into the Siemens-Greece case to the first court verdict (2019) — which was then largely overturned on appeal three years later. [GreekReporter, Nov 2019](https://greekreporter.com/2019/11/19/greek-court-finds-22-guilty-in-siemens-bribery-trial/)

## 5. Language of denial

Real, sourced quotations from Greek political-corruption episodes — denial, minimization, or defiance:

1. **Vasilis Papageorgopoulos**, former Mayor of Thessaloniki, on being convicted and sentenced to life imprisonment for the €18 million municipal embezzlement scheme (2013): *"I have nothing to do with this case. I'm sure that some people will die of guilt."* He also called the ruling *"of a political nature, aimed exclusively at satisfying a public that is demanding political blood,"* and said he was sure *"no Greek believes that Vassilis Papageorgopoulos betrayed them."* [ProtoThema English, "Former Thessaloniki mayor's life sentence reduced to 12 years"](https://en.protothema.gr/former-thessaloniki-mayors-life-sentence-reduced-to-12-years)

2. **Akis Tsochatzopoulos**, former Minister of National Defence, on his release from prison in 2017 (sentence later finalized at 19 years): *"My torture is over,"* while blaming former PASOK prime ministers Costas Simitis and George Papandreou for orchestrating his prosecution. [GreekReporter, "Former Defense Minister Tsochatzopoulos Released from Prison"](https://greece.greekreporter.com/2017/05/04/former-defense-minister-tsochatzopoulos-released-from-prison/)

3. **Makis Voridis**, on resigning as Minister of Migration and Asylum during the OPEKEPE farm-subsidy scandal (June 2025): *"In order to devote myself fully to what is most important to me, defending my innocence before Parliament, as well as protecting my honor and reputation, I hereby submit my resignation."* He also told the parliamentary investigative committee: *"I never intervened in any taxpayer or farm subsidy case."* [The National Herald](https://www.thenationalherald.com/two-more-top-greek-officials-resign-over-subsidies-scandal/) | [Athens Times](https://athens-times.com/voridis-denies-political-pressure-in-opekepe-scandal-hearing/)

4. **Eva Kaili**, on the Qatargate corruption allegations, through her lawyer Sven Mary, who coined the counter-term "Belgiangate" to characterize what they called a flawed investigation; Kaili has *"denied all charges"* throughout. (Included here as a documented denial quote; note per Section 2.7 that her case remains unresolved and she is not named as convicted or resigned in this dossier's substantive entries.) [Euronews, Dec 2022, "Eva Kaili lawyer claims her innocence in Qatargate corruption scandal"](https://www.euronews.com/my-europe/2022/12/21/eva-kaili-lawyer-claims-her-innocence-in-qatargate-corruption-scandal)

5. **Kyriakos Mitsotakis**, Prime Minister, denying personal responsibility for the Predator spyware surveillance scandal (November 2022) — a surveillance rather than bribery scandal, included for its documented and widely-quoted denial: *"There is absolutely no evidence and there is no connection with me."* Government spokesperson Giannis Oikonomou separately told the New York Times: *"The Greek authorities and security services have at no time acquired or used the Predator surveillance software. To suggest otherwise is wrong."* [Balkan Insight, Nov 2022](https://balkaninsight.com/2022/11/08/greek-pm-shame-and-disgrace-to-be-accused-of-spying-on-ministers/)
