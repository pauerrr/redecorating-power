# Hungary — Political Corruption Dossier

*Compiled for a Domestic Data Streamers workshop. Sources are cited inline. Individuals are named only where they meet a strict legal-inclusion rule (see note below). This is a research compilation for educational/journalistic purposes, comparable to Transparency International or OCCRP country profiles.*

**Inclusion rule applied throughout:** a person is named as "convicted" only if they hold a **final** criminal conviction, upheld by Hungary's final appellate instance, for a corruption-related offense, or is named as "resigned" only if they resigned from office over an extensively documented scandal — labelled explicitly as "resigned, not convicted." People who are only accused, charged, under active trial, or acquitted are described by role/institution only, with the legal status stated explicitly. For the EU-funds-to-connected-companies material, this dossier describes a **system**, using institutional findings (OLAF final reports, the European Commission, the European Court of Auditors, the EU's rule-of-law conditionality mechanism) attributed explicitly to those institutions — this is not an allegation that any named individual committed a crime.

## 1. Country snapshot

- **Transparency International Corruption Perceptions Index (CPI) 2024** (published 11 Feb 2025): Hungary scored **41/100**, ranking **82nd of 180 countries** — down from 76th in 2023. Transparency International states this marks **"the third year in a row [Hungary] retained its ranking as the EU's most corrupt country."** [Transparency.org — Hungary 2024](https://www.transparency.org/en/cpi/2024/index/hun) | [TI Hungary, CPI 2024 annual report](https://transparency.hu/en/news/cpi-2024-results-annual-report/)
- **CPI 2023:** Hungary scored **42/100**, ranked 76th of 180 — already the lowest score in the EU that year. [Transparency.org — Hungary 2023](https://www.transparency.org/en/cpi/2023/index/hun)
- **Eurobarometer (2024):** **88%** of Hungarians say corruption is widespread in the country, above the EU average of 68%; in the parallel Flash Eurobarometer business survey, **75–78%** of Hungarian companies say corruption is widespread in business, versus 63–65% EU-wide. [Euronews fact-check, Oct 2024](https://www.euronews.com/my-europe/2024/10/15/fact-check-is-hungary-the-most-corrupt-european-country) | [European Commission Eurobarometer survey portal](https://europa.eu/eurobarometer/surveys/detail/3217)
- **EU funds frozen — rule-of-law conditionality mechanism:** the European Commission proposed suspending **€7.5 billion** on 18 September 2022; the Council of the EU decided on **16 December 2022** to suspend **€6.3 billion** — the first-ever use of Regulation 2020/2092 against any member state. A further **€10.4 billion** in Recovery and Resilience Facility (RRF) funds was blocked pending "27 super milestones" on judicial independence. [Council of the EU press release, 16 Dec 2022](https://www.consilium.europa.eu/en/press/press-releases/2022/12/12/rule-of-law-conditionality-mechanism/) | [eucrim, "Commission Triggers Conditionality Mechanism Against Hungary"](https://eucrim.eu/news/commission-triggers-conditionality-mechanism-against-hungary/)
- On **13 December 2023**, the Commission released roughly **€10.2 billion** on a separate legal track tied to judicial-reform milestones, while explicitly maintaining the €6.3 billion conditionality-mechanism freeze. The European Parliament sued the Commission at the EU Court of Justice (Case C-225/24) on 25 March 2024 over this release, arguing it was premature; an Advocate General's opinion (12 Feb 2026) proposed the Court annul the decision, with judgment still pending. [European Commission press release, Dec 2023](https://ec.europa.eu/commission/presscorner/detail/en/ip_23_6465) | [CJEU press release on AG opinion, Feb 2026](https://curia.europa.eu/site/upload/docs/application/pdf/2026-02/cp260015en.pdf)
- At the end of 2024, Hungary **permanently lost approximately €1.04 billion** in cohesion funds after an EU absorption deadline lapsed. As of the Commission's 8 July 2025 Rule of Law Report, **€18 billion remains frozen** (roughly €8.4bn in cohesion funds plus €9.5bn in RRF/COVID-recovery funds). EU Justice Commissioner Michael McGrath said: *"It is deeply disappointing we're not in a position to report further progress on the recommendations that were made last year."* [TI Hungary, CPI 2024 report](https://transparency.hu/en/news/cpi-2024-results-annual-report/) | [Euronews, 8 Jul 2025](https://www.euronews.com/my-europe/2025/07/08/eu-will-keep-18-billion-frozen-for-hungary-after-no-progress-on-rule-of-law-concerns)
- **GRECO (Council of Europe):** Hungary has been under a **non-compliance procedure since June 2017**. GRECO's Fourth Round evaluation found Hungary's implementation rate had fallen to **53%**, down from 90% after the first compliance report. Its Fifth Round Evaluation Report (adopted June 2022, published June 2023) found **"the integrity framework applicable to persons with top executive functions is very weak."** [Council of Europe, GRECO 5th Round/4th Round interim report, 2023](https://www.coe.int/en/web/greco/-/hungary-publication-of-5th-round-evaluation-report-and-4th-interim-compliance-report-of-4th-round)
- **European Court of Auditors:** Special Report 03/2024 found the Commission's 2022 fund freeze "justified" on the facts, but warned that the mechanism risks becoming "a box-ticking exercise" and noted the timing of the December 2023 partial release coincided with a Council vote on Ukraine's EU-accession process. A separate ECA special report on conflict of interest (06/2023) flagged that Hungary's "KEKVA" public-interest asset-management trusts are structured to sit outside standard EU procurement and conflict-of-interest rules. [ECA Special Report 03/2024](https://www.eca.europa.eu/en/publications/SR-2024-03) | [ECA Special Report 06/2023 on conflict of interest](https://op.europa.eu/webpub/eca/special-reports/conflict-of-interest-06-2023/en/)

## 2. Major cases and systems

### 2.1 The Elios Innovative streetlighting scheme — OLAF findings, domestic case closed without charges (2011–2018)

**Institutions:** Municipal public-lighting procurement across dozens of Hungarian towns; EU Cohesion and Regional Development funds; the European Anti-Fraud Office (OLAF); the Hungarian National Bureau of Investigation.

**What OLAF found:** OLAF's final report (finalized around December 2017, partially made public through subsequent litigation) examined 35 EU-funded street-lighting modernization tenders won by the company **Elios Innovative**, and found irregularities in **all 35** of them, with what it described as an **"organized fraud mechanism"** ensuring only Elios could realistically win in **17 of the 35**. OLAF's report identified **€43.7 million** in implicated EU funding (projects were 85–100% EU-financed) and recommended the **full recovery of the €43.7 million**. [OCCRP, "Hungary: Serious Irregularities in Public Lighting Contracts"](https://www.occrp.org/en/news/hungary-serious-irregularities-in-public-lighting-contracts) | [Transparency International Hungary briefing note on Elios](https://transparency.hu/en/news/ti-hungarys-briefing-note-on-the-case-of-the-elios-company/)

**Individuals referenced in OLAF's findings — not charged, not convicted, named here only as OLAF's own attributed findings:** OLAF's report identified **István Tiborcz**, at the time son-in-law of Prime Minister Viktor Orbán and a co-owner/manager of Elios Innovative until May 2015, and **Endre Hamar**, Tiborcz's business partner and co-owner of Elios, whose separate consultancy had drafted the technical tender specifications — flagged by OLAF as a conflict of interest. Neither individual was ever charged with a crime in Hungary. This dossier reports these names strictly as content of OLAF's institutional findings, not as an allegation of criminal guilt. [OCCRP](https://www.occrp.org/en/news/hungary-serious-irregularities-in-public-lighting-contracts) | [TI Hungary briefing note](https://transparency.hu/en/news/ti-hungarys-briefing-note-on-the-case-of-the-elios-company/)

**Domestic outcome:** Hungarian authorities closed an initial domestic inquiry in 2015–2016; after OLAF's report prompted a reopening in January 2018, the National Police Headquarters closed the case again on **7 November 2018**, concluding that **"no criminal activity occurred."** The Hungarian government publicly dismissed OLAF's report as "a mere political campaign-report from Brussels." [Index.hu (English), 7 Nov 2018](https://index.hu/english/2018/11/07/elios_investigation_closed/)

**EU-level follow-up:** in February 2019, Hungary declined to submit the invoices the Commission had requested for the flagged contracts; in April 2019 the Commission conducted a cartel-related inspection at former Elios premises; the General Court of the EU later ruled that OLAF and the Commission had wrongly refused to make the full report public. No individual associated with Elios has ever faced a criminal charge in Hungary in connection with this scheme. [TI Hungary briefing note](https://transparency.hu/en/news/ti-hungarys-briefing-note-on-the-case-of-the-elios-company/) | [VSquare, "Hungarian NGO Wins: OLAF's Elios Report Must Be Made Public"](https://vsquare.org/hungarian-ngo-wins-olafs-elios-report-must-be-made-public/)

### 2.2 The EU rule-of-law conditionality mechanism and systemic fund mismanagement — an institutional system, not an individual case

**Institutions:** European Commission; Council of the European Union; European Court of Auditors; European Court of Justice; OLAF.

**The system, described through institutional findings:** Since 2022, the EU has run its first-ever application of the rule-of-law conditionality regulation (2020/2092) against a member state — Hungary — citing systemic weaknesses in judicial independence and public procurement oversight that put EU funds at risk of misuse. The Council's **16 December 2022** decision suspended **€6.3 billion**; a further **€10.4 billion** in Recovery and Resilience Facility funds has been withheld pending judicial-reform milestones. By July 2025, the Commission reported **€18 billion still frozen**, and Hungary had already permanently forfeited roughly **€1.04 billion** at the end of 2024 through a lapsed absorption deadline. [Council of the EU, Dec 2022](https://www.consilium.europa.eu/en/press/press-releases/2022/12/12/rule-of-law-conditionality-mechanism/) | [Euronews, Jul 2025](https://www.euronews.com/my-europe/2025/07/08/eu-will-keep-18-billion-frozen-for-hungary-after-no-progress-on-rule-of-law-concerns)

**OLAF's own recovery-recommendation statistics single out Hungary systemically:** according to OLAF's own annual reporting, Hungary had the **highest OLAF-recommended-recovery rate relative to EU payments received of any member state**: **3.84%** of Hungary's EU payments were flagged for recovery over 2014–2018 against an EU average of **0.45%**, and **3.93%** against a **0.36%** average over 2015–2019. Separately, over the period 2015–2024, Hungary is reported to have recovered only **18%** of the **€1.39 billion** in EU funds that OLAF flagged as irregular. [EUobserver, "Hungary Tops EU Anti-Fraud Investigation List"](https://euobserver.com/70738/hungary-tops-eu-anti-fraud-investigation-list) | [Forbes, citing OLAF data](https://www.forbes.com/sites/niallmccarthy/2020/09/14/hungary-tops-european-anti-fraud-office-list-for-eu-financial-irregularities-infographic/) | [Agenzia Nova, recovery-rate figure](https://www.agenzianova.com/en/news/Hungary-returned-only-18-percent-of-funds-reported-by-the-EU-Anti-Fraud-Office-between-2015-and-2024./)

**European Court of Auditors findings on structural conflict-of-interest exposure:** the ECA's special report on conflict of interest (06/2023) found that Hungary's system of "KEKVA" public-interest asset-management foundations/trusts — vehicles that now hold large amounts of formerly state or EU-linked public assets — are structured in a way that **exempts them from standard EU procurement directives and conflict-of-interest safeguards**, an institutional structural finding rather than an allegation against any named individual. [ECA Special Report 06/2023](https://op.europa.eu/webpub/eca/special-reports/conflict-of-interest-06-2023/en/)

**Single-bidder procurement — Corruption Research Center Budapest (CRCB):** the European Commission's own Single Market Scoreboard recorded Hungary's public-procurement single-bidder rate at **34%** against an EU benchmark of **28%** (2024 data). CRCB research (Research Note 2022/3, by István János Tóth) documents that the EU considers a single-bidder rate below 10% desirable, and that EU-funded contracts with more than three bidders made up only **29%** (2020) and **33%** (2021) of all such contracts. The same CRCB analysis found that **42 companies tied to 12 Fidesz-connected owners** — including Lőrinc Mészáros and István Tiborcz — won **21% of the net value of EU-funded public contracts awarded in Hungary between 2011 and 2021** (excluding framework agreements), with a win-odds ratio of **3.2**, compared with roughly **0.5** for unconnected firms. [European Commission Single Market Scoreboard](https://single-market-scoreboard.ec.europa.eu/node/526_en) | [CRCB Research Notes 2022:3 (PDF)](https://www.crcb.eu/wp-content/uploads/2022/03/2022_research_notes_03_220307_02.pdf)

**Why this is presented as a system, not a case:** none of the companies or individuals named in this section have been criminally charged or convicted in relation to these findings. The figures above are institutional statistical and audit findings (OLAF, European Commission, European Court of Auditors, CRCB academic research), reported here as descriptions of a procurement and fund-allocation system, not as allegations of individual criminal conduct.

### 2.3 The Völner–Schadl bribery case — no verdict reached; resignation only

**Institutions:** Ministry of Justice; the Hungarian Chamber of Judicial Officers (bailiffs).

**Status: this case has NOT produced a final verdict against its two principal defendants and cannot be reported as a conviction.** Former Justice Ministry state secretary **Pál Völner** and **György Schadl**, former deputy head of the Hungarian Chamber of Judicial Officers, were charged in connection with an alleged scheme in which bailiff appointments and favorable treatment were exchanged for bribes. Trial opened at the Budapest Metropolitan Court (Fővárosi Törvényszék) on **17 February 2023**; both men have pleaded not guilty. As of the most recent confirmed reporting (witness hearings continuing into April 2026), **not even a first-instance verdict has been reached** against Völner or Schadl. [Telex (English), "The Biggest Corruption Case of Recent Times in Hungary," 9 Mar 2023](https://telex.hu/english/2023/03/09/the-biggest-corruption-case-of-recent-times-in-hungary-the-schadl-volner-case) | [Index.hu, witness hearing coverage, 28 Apr 2026](https://index.hu/belfold/2026/04/28/schadl-gyorgy-volner-pal-fovarosi-torvenyszek-tanumeghallgatas-targyalas-korrupcio-hivatali-vesztegetes/)

**What IS documented and usable:** Völner **resigned** as Justice Ministry state secretary on **8 December 2021** as the scandal broke — a confirmed resignation, separate from the still-unresolved criminal trial. [Wikipedia, Pál Völner, cross-referenced against contemporary Hungarian press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P%C3%A1l_V%C3%B6lner)

**Separate, smaller final convictions in the same case file:** roughly ten lower-level bailiffs charged as co-defendants in the same case admitted guilt and accepted suspended sentences; the Budapest Metropolitan Court of Appeal reduced and finalized their sentences (suspended prison terms of 18–24 months, five-year bans from bailiff work, and asset forfeiture in some cases) on **8 February 2024**. These are minor co-defendants, not Völner or Schadl, and their conviction does not establish guilt for the two named principals. [Infostart.hu, 8 Feb 2024](https://infostart.hu/bunugyek/2024/02/08/schadl-volner-ugy-csokkentettek-az-erintettseguket-beismero-vegrehajtok-bunteteset)

**Per the strict inclusion rule, neither Völner nor Schadl is listed as "convicted" in this dossier's People Index; only Völner's December 2021 resignation is reportable.**

### 2.4 Pál Schmitt — resignation over a plagiarism scandal (2012)

**Institution:** The Presidency of Hungary; Semmelweis University (formerly the University of Physical Education).

**The scandal:** Hungary's President **Pál Schmitt** had his 1992 doctoral thesis on the modern Olympic Games found to contain approximately **197 of 215 pages** lifted or translated, largely without attribution, from the work of Bulgarian IOC official Nikolai Georgiev and German sports historian Klaus Heinemann. [Wikipedia, Pál Schmitt academic misconduct controversy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P%C3%A1l_Schmitt_academic_misconduct_controversy)

**Institutional and personal outcome:** Semmelweis University's Senate voted, by a reported **33–4 margin**, to revoke Schmitt's doctorate on **29 March 2012**. Schmitt **resigned as President of Hungary on 2 April 2012**, becoming the first Hungarian head of state to resign since the fall of communism. This is a resignation, not a criminal conviction — plagiarism is an academic-integrity matter rather than a corruption offense, but the case is included here as an extensively documented resignation scandal at the highest level of the Hungarian state. [France24, 2 Apr 2012](https://www.france24.com/en/20120402-hungary-president-resigns-plagiarism-scandal-pal-schmitt-dissertation) | [CNN, 2 Apr 2012](https://edition.cnn.com/2012/04/02/world/europe/hungary-president-resigns)

### 2.5 The Miklós Hagyó case — final conviction (Budapest transport company embezzlement)

**Institution:** Budapesti Közlekedési Zrt. (BKV), Budapest's public transport company.

**The case:** **Miklós Hagyó**, former Deputy Mayor of Budapest (MSZP), was tried on charges of abuse of office and embezzlement connected to BKV. The **Szeged Court of Appeal delivered a final verdict on 29 September 2017**, sentencing Hagyó to **1.5 years' imprisonment, suspended for 3 years**, plus an order to pay more than **160 million forints** in damages. [HVG, 29 Sep 2017](https://hvg.hu/itthon/20170929_jogerosen_is_felfuggesztett_bortont_kapott_hagyo_miklos)

**Legal status: final conviction, confirmed 2017.**

### 2.6 Katalin Novák and Judit Varga — resignations over the presidential pardon scandal (2024)

**Institution:** The Presidency of Hungary; Ministry of Justice.

**The scandal:** In February 2024 it emerged that President **Katalin Novák** had granted a presidential pardon to a man convicted of covering up sexual abuse at a children's home by pressuring the victims to withdraw testimony against the institution's director. Public outrage followed.

**Outcome:** Novák **resigned as President on 10 February 2024**, and then-Justice Minister **Judit Varga**, who had countersigned the pardon, **resigned in parallel**. Neither has been criminally charged; this is reported strictly as a resignation over a documented scandal, not a corruption conviction, but it is one of the most consequential resignations in recent Hungarian political history and is included for completeness. [PBS NewsHour / AP, 10 Feb 2024](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/world/hungarys-president-resigns-after-pardoning-man-convicted-in-child-sexual-abuse-case)

## 6. Methodology and workshop-use notes

**On sourcing:** every factual claim above carries an inline citation. Institutional sources (Transparency International, OLAF, the European Commission, the European Court of Auditors, GRECO, CRCB) are cited to their own publications wherever those were retrievable; where only high-quality secondary reporting on an institutional document was available (e.g. some OLAF report details, only summarized in OCCRP/TI Hungary coverage since the full report was for years withheld from publication), that is noted explicitly.

**On the "system vs. case" distinction:** Section 2.2 deliberately avoids naming any individual as having committed a crime in connection with EU-funds allocation patterns. The Elios case (2.1) is the one exception where individuals are named — but strictly as subjects of OLAF's own institutional findings, with explicit notice that no criminal charge was ever filed against them in Hungary. Facilitators using this dossier in a workshop setting should preserve this distinction: statistical over-representation of connected firms in procurement outcomes is not equivalent to proof of individual wrongdoing, and this dossier does not claim otherwise.

**On pending cases:** the Völner–Schadl case (2.3) is the clearest illustration in this dossier of how slowly high-profile corruption prosecutions can move in Hungary — more than three years elapsed between the original December 2021 resignation and the point where, per Völner's own court submission, "not even a first-instance verdict" existed. Facilitators may wish to use this specific delay as a discussion prompt about the gap between a corruption scandal's political consequences (an immediate resignation) and its judicial consequences (which may take years, or may never arrive).

**On EU findings versus domestic outcomes:** the Elios case is also a useful illustration of a structural pattern documented across this project's dossiers — an EU institutional body (OLAF) reaching a strong factual finding (fraud mechanisms in 17 of 35 tenders, full recovery recommended) that is then not acted upon by the domestic justice system (case closed twice, no charges, no recovery). This gap between EU-level fact-finding and domestic prosecutorial follow-through is itself one of the "numbers that sting" in Section 4 (the 18% OLAF-fund-recovery rate) and is worth foregrounding in any workshop material built from this dossier.
