# Italy — Documented Political Corruption Dossier

*Compiled for the Domestic Data Streamers workshop repository. This is an educational/journalistic compilation of public-record Italian legal cases, in the spirit of Transparency International and OCCRP reporting. Every named individual either (a) received a FINAL, definitive criminal conviction (passed "in giudicato") for a corruption-related offense, or (b) resigned from public office over a documented scandal — clearly labeled "resigned, not convicted." People who were only investigated, indicted, acquitted, or whose cases lapsed under Italy's statute of limitations (prescrizione) are described by role/title only, never named as guilty.*

## 1. Country Snapshot

**Corruption Perceptions Index (Transparency International).** Italy scored **53/100** in the CPI 2024, ranking **52nd of 180 countries** — down from **56/100** in CPI 2023, a three-point year-on-year decline. Italy sits below the Western Europe & EU regional average (around 62–65) and roughly **19th of 27 EU member states**. [Transparency.org — CPI 2024](https://www.transparency.org/en/cpi/2024) · [Transparency.org — Italy country page](https://www.transparency.org/en/countries/italy) · [Transparency.org — CPI 2023](https://www.transparency.org/en/cpi/2023) · [Il Sole 24 Ore — Italy ranks 52nd](https://en.ilsole24ore.com/art/corruption-two-third-countries-are-risk-italy-52-place-AIvdODJ)

**Eurobarometer — perceived corruption.** In the **2023 Special Eurobarometer on corruption** (fieldwork March–May 2023, EU-wide n=26,404), **92% of Italian respondents** said corruption is widespread in Italy — among the highest shares in the EU, close to Greece (95%) and Romania (92%). [Anticor.world — 2023 Eurobarometer findings](https://anticor.world/en/main/news_page/findings_of_2023_eurobarometer_released) · [European Commission — 2023 corruption surveys](https://home-affairs.ec.europa.eu/news/commissions-2023-surveys-corruption-show-growing-scepticism-among-europeans-2023-07-05_en). The companion **2024 Flash Eurobarometer on businesses** found **29% of Italian companies** say corruption stopped them winning a public tender in the previous three years. [Europa.eu — Eurobarometer survey 3180](https://europa.eu/eurobarometer/surveys/detail/3180)

**The "€60 billion a year" figure — contested, not a verified statistic.** This number is ubiquitous in Italian media but its provenance is weak and it should **not** be treated as a rigorous empirical estimate. It traces back to a mis-application of a **2004 World Bank global bribery study** (which estimated worldwide bribery at ~3% of global GDP): someone applied that global percentage directly to Italian GDP, producing "€50–60 billion." Italy's own anti-corruption service at the time, **SAeT** (Servizio Anticorruzione e Trasparenza, Department of Public Administration), floated the figure in a 2009 report, then **formally retracted it in 2010 as a "stima fantasiosa"** (fanciful estimate). The **Corte dei Conti** (Court of Auditors) referenced the same range in a 2009 address — which is likely the origin of the popular but imprecise claim that "the head of Italy's Court of Auditors said corruption costs €60bn/year" — and the Corte dei Conti itself criticized the figure again in a 2012 report. Despite this, the number was picked up uncritically by the European Commission's 2014 EU Anti-Corruption Report, prompting further fact-checks. **Treat "€60 billion/year" as a debunked or, at best, heavily contested figure — not a hard fact.** [Il Sole 24 Ore — "La leggenda dei 60 miliardi"](https://st.ilsole24ore.com/art/notizie/2016-08-20/la-leggenda-60-miliardi-stima-falsa-che-tutti-citano-185856.shtml) · [Pagella Politica — fact-check](https://pagellapolitica.it/fact-checking/corruzione-quanto-ci-costi-la-bufala-dei-60-miliardi) · [AGI — fact-check](https://www.agi.it/fact-checking/la_maxi_bufala_della_corruzione_che_ci_costa_60_miliardi_lanno_e_che_tutti_ripetono_dal_2009-1302658/news/2016-12-09/) · [ANSA — the €60bn case and the EU alarm](https://www.ansa.it/web/notizie/rubriche/politica/2014/02/03/Corruzione-Italia-vale-60-miliardi_10007850.html) · [Il Fatto Quotidiano — Corte dei Conti criticism, 2012](https://www.ilfattoquotidiano.it/2012/02/16/evasione-italia-livelli-alti-europa-dilagano-illegalita-corruzione-malaffare/191661/)

**GRECO (Group of States against Corruption, Council of Europe).** GRECO's **Fifth Round Evaluation Report on Italy** (2024), covering corruption prevention in top executive government functions and law enforcement, found Italy has a "sizeable legal framework… but complicated to navigate, to the detriment of its efficiency," with no common integrity framework across top government roles. GRECO issued **19 recommendations** (split between top officials and law enforcement agencies), including calls for dedicated codes of conduct, confidential integrity counselling, and better gender diversity in police leadership. Italy was required to report back on implementation by **30 September 2025**. Separately, Italy is still completing compliance steps from the **Fourth Evaluation Round** (MPs, judges, prosecutors); a Second Addendum to that round's Second Compliance Report was published in **March 2024**. [Council of Europe/GRECO — press release](https://www.coe.int/en/web/greco/-/greco-urges-public-authorities-to-be-exemplary-and-transparent-new-report-by-council-of-europe-s-anti-corruption-body) · [Eucrim — Fifth Round report on Italy](https://eucrim.eu/news/greco-fifth-round-evaluation-report-on-italy/) · [JURIST](https://www.jurist.org/news/2024/08/council-of-europe-calls-on-italy-to-strengthen-anti-corruption-measures/) · [Council of Europe — Fourth Round Addendum](https://www.coe.int/en/web/greco/-/italy-publication-of-the-4th-evaluation-round-addendum-to-the-2nd-compliance-report)

**OECD Anti-Bribery Convention.** The OECD's **2024 Phase 4 Two-Year Follow-Up Report** found Italy had fully implemented only 18 of its recommendations, partially implemented 7, and left 23 not implemented. It flagged that of eight foreign-bribery trials since a single 2013 conviction, five ended in full dismissal and one in partial dismissal — only one further conviction resulted — and that most effective enforcement touching Italian conduct has come from **US FCPA prosecutions**, not Italian courts. [OECD — Italy country monitoring](https://www.oecd.org/en/topics/sub-issues/fighting-foreign-bribery/italy-country-monitoring.html) · [OECD Phase 4 Follow-Up Report, PDF](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2024/10/implementing-the-oecd-anti-bribery-convention-phase-4-two-year-follow-up-report-italy_65d235f7/5841d4d2-en.pdf) · [National Law Review](https://natlawreview.com/article/italys-failure-uphold-anti-bribery-convention-exposed-oecd-report)

## 2. Major Cases

### 2.1 Tangentopoli / Mani Pulite ("Clean Hands"), 1992–1996

**Institutions involved:** Italian Socialist Party (PSI), Christian Democracy (DC), Italian Democratic Socialist Party (PSDI), Italian Liberal Party (PLI), state-owned conglomerates (IRI, ENI, ENEL), Lega Nord.

**Mechanism:** Systemic kickbacks ("tangenti") on public-works contracts, illegal party financing, and "lottizzazione" — the allotment of top jobs at state-controlled companies to party loyalists in exchange for cash flowing back to party coffers.

**Trigger and scale:** The investigation began **17 February 1992**, when Milan magistrate Antonio Di Pietro had PSI official Mario Chiesa arrested for a 7-million-lire bribe. In secondary accounts, "as many as 5,000 public figures" fell under suspicion, and by the end of 1992 alone roughly 4,800 people had been arrested, with over 620 billion lire in bribes traced by police that year; more than 1,300 people were eventually convicted or plea-bargained nationwide. [Wikipedia — Mani pulite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mani_pulite) · [GlobalSecurity.org](https://www.globalsecurity.org/military/world/europe/it-clean-hands.htm) · [The Conversation — Italy's horrible year](https://theconversation.com/looking-back-at-1992-italys-horrible-year-66739)

**Convicted — Bettino Craxi** (PSI leader, Prime Minister of Italy 1983–1987). Of roughly seven proceedings he faced, only **two produced sentences that became legally final (passed in giudicato)**:
- **ENI-SAI corruption case**: 5 years 6 months, confirmed by the Corte di Cassazione, final **12–13 November 1996**.
- **Metropolitana Milanese case** (Milan subway contracts, illegal party financing): 4 years 6 months, final **20–21 April 1999**.
- **Total legally final sentence: 10 years.** (Note: some outlets, including BBC, cite a cumulative "27 years" or "9 years 8 months" — these figures loosely sum sentences from cases that were later annulled, time-barred, or never reached a final Cassazione ruling before Craxi's death. Only the 10 years above is supported as definitively final.)
Craxi fled to Hammamet, Tunisia in May 1994 and was formally declared a fugitive from Italian justice in July 1995; both final convictions became res judicata after he was already abroad. He died in Hammamet on **19 January 2000**, aged 65, never having served his sentence in Italy. [it.wikipedia.org — Bettino Craxi](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bettino_Craxi) · [Il Fatto Quotidiano, 2017](https://www.ilfattoquotidiano.it/2017/01/20/bettino-craxi-tangenti-per-miliardi-a-domicilio-ecco-perche-fu-condannato/3329146/) · [Irish Times, 3 May 1996](https://www.irishtimes.com/news/court-upholds-craxi-sentence-1.45607) · [BBC News obituary](https://news.bbc.co.uk/1/hi/world/europe/610659.stm)

**Other final convictions from Tangentopoli:** **Severino Citaristi**, DC national treasurer, convicted definitively (16 years cumulative plus over 8 billion lire in fines); **Sergio Cusani**, financier who distributed the roughly 150-billion-lire Enimont bribe, convicted definitively to 5 years 10 months (Cassazione, 21 January 1998); **Giuseppe Garofano**, Montedison chairman, convicted definitively to 3 years (Cassazione, June 1998); **Duilio Poggiolini**, Health Ministry official in the parallel "Farmacopoli" pharmaceutical bribery affair, convicted definitively to 4 years 4 months (separately and unrelatedly acquitted in 2019 of a different, non-corruption charge concerning infected blood products — the two cases must not be conflated). [it.wikipedia.org — Processo Enimont](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Processo_Enimont) · [it.wikipedia.org — Severino Citaristi](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Severino_Citaristi) · [it.wikipedia.org — Duilio Poggiolini](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duilio_Poggiolini)

**Not counted as convicted:** Several other prominent Tangentopoli figures saw most of their cases end in acquittal or via statute-of-limitations expiry (prescrizione) rather than final conviction, and are referred to here by role only where relevant. Italian Communist Party (PCI) officials investigated during Tangentopoli were, per Wikipedia's account, "largely acquitted."

**Aftermath:** The DC and PSI both dissolved in 1994; the PSI's vote share collapsed from 13.6% (1992) to 2.2% (1994). Italy adopted a new, largely majoritarian electoral law (the "Mattarellum") in August 1993 after an April 1993 referendum. Silvio Berlusconi founded Forza Italia in December 1993 and won the 1994 election — conventionally marking the transition from Italy's "First Republic" party system to its "Second Republic." [Wikipedia — Mani pulite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mani_pulite) · [Wikipedia — Italian electoral law of 1993](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_electoral_law_of_1993) · [Wikipedia — Forza Italia (1994)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forza_Italia_(1994))

### 2.2 Berlusconi Mediaset Tax Fraud, conduct 1994–1998 / final ruling 2013

**Institution involved:** Mediaset/Fininvest (Silvio Berlusconi's media conglomerate).

**Mechanism:** Mediaset purchased US film and TV broadcasting rights through offshore intermediary companies in tax havens, which bought rights cheaply and resold them to Mediaset at artificially inflated prices. This generated fictitious losses in Italy (reducing taxable profit) while the margin accumulated, untaxed, in offshore vehicles ultimately controlled by insiders. Total scheme value was estimated at roughly **€470 million**; Berlusconi personally was said to have profited by more than **€270 million**. His own tax-evasion charge was quantified at **€7.3 million**. [Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/afontevecchia/2012/10/26/berlusconis-tax-evasion-using-offshore-accounts-to-make-349m-on-u-s-tv-and-movie-rights/) · [France24](https://www.france24.com/en/20130508-italy-berlusconi-tax-fraud-conviction-upheld-four-years-prison)

**Convicted — Silvio Berlusconi.** Milan Tribunal convicted him on **26 October 2012** (4 years); the Milan Court of Appeal upheld it on **8 May 2013**; the **Corte di Cassazione made it final on 1 August 2013** — Berlusconi's first-ever definitive conviction after roughly two decades of trials. Three of the four years were wiped out under Italy's **2006 indulto** (a general clemency law), leaving one year to serve; as an over-70 defendant, he served it via **community service**, ordered on 15 April 2014, assisting Alzheimer's patients four hours a week at the Fondazione Sacra Famiglia in Cesano Boscone, near Milan. Under the "Severino Law" anti-corruption statute (passed December 2012), the Senate formally expelled him from his seat on **27 November 2013**, applying a **six-year ban from public office**. The Surveillance Tribunal of Milan lifted the ban early via "riabilitazione" on **12 May 2018**, and Berlusconi was subsequently elected to the European Parliament in May 2019, serving until his death in June 2023. [France24, 8 May 2013](https://www.france24.com/en/20130508-italy-berlusconi-tax-fraud-conviction-upheld-four-years-prison) · [France24, 1 Aug 2013](https://www.france24.com/en/20130801-italy-top-court-upholds-berlusconi-tax-fraud-conviction) · [Il Sole 24 Ore — community service order](https://st.ilsole24ore.com/art/notizie/2014-04-15/processo-mediaset-berlusconi-affidato-prova-servizi-sociali--114537.shtml) · [NPR — community service](https://www.npr.org/sections/thetwo-way/2014/04/15/303202554/berlusconi-ordered-to-do-community-service-at-senior-center) · [Al Jazeera — ban lifted, 2018](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2018/5/12/italy-tribunal-lifts-ban-on-berlusconi-holding-public-office) · [Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Silvio-Berlusconi/Prosecutions-political-ban-and-continued-influence)

**Not counted as convicted (context only):** Berlusconi faced numerous other trials — including the "Ruby" underage-prostitution/abuse-of-office case (convicted at first instance in 2013, but **acquitted on appeal in 2014**, with the acquittal made final by Cassazione on 10 March 2015) and several bribery/false-accounting cases that expired under Italy's statute of limitations before a final ruling. These ended without conviction and are not counted here. [Wikipedia — Silvio Berlusconi prostitution trial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silvio_Berlusconi_prostitution_trial) · [Il Post — Ruby final acquittal](https://www.ilpost.it/2015/03/10/sentenza-cassazione-assoluzione-berlusconi-ruby/)

**Aftermath:** The looming conviction contributed to the collapse of the Monti technocratic government and a hung parliament after the February 2013 election. Berlusconi's PdL later split, with the more moderate wing forming the New Centre-Right under Angelino Alfano, while Berlusconi relaunched Forza Italia. [Wikipedia — Controversies surrounding Silvio Berlusconi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Controversies_surrounding_Silvio_Berlusconi)

### 2.3 MOSE Venice Flood-Barrier Scandal, investigation from 2014

**Institutions involved:** Consorzio Venezia Nuova (CVN, sole concessionaire for the project), Veneto regional government, Comune di Venezia.

**Mechanism:** MOSE ("Modulo Sperimentale Elettromeccanico") is the mobile flood-barrier system built to protect Venice from high tides. Prosecutors alleged CVN used inflated invoices to extract excess public funding, routed surplus money offshore (including via San Marino), and used it to bribe officials to speed approvals, weaken Court of Auditors oversight, and illegally finance political campaigns across party lines. Arrests began **4 June 2014**, netting roughly 35 people, including sitting Venice mayor Giorgio Orsoni. Total project cost reached roughly **€6.2 billion**, about double the original budget; one specific laundering channel moved roughly **€20 million (~$27 million)** through San Marino accounts, while broader fraud/bribery estimates across the whole scheme run considerably higher (reported up to roughly $276 million) — these are different, non-interchangeable figures and should not be conflated. [OCCRP](https://www.occrp.org/en/news/italy-mayor-of-venice-arrested-for-corruption) · [CGTN](https://newseu.cgtn.com/news/2019-11-16/What-s-happened-to-Venice-s-MOSE-flood-defences--LE66pVsGas/index.html) · [corruptiondata.eu](https://corruptiondata.eu/the-mose-scandal-in-venice/)

**Convicted — Giancarlo Galan**, former President of the Veneto Region and former Italian Minister of Agriculture, accused of receiving roughly €1 million/year from CVN between 2005–2008 and again in 2012. He accepted a **patteggiamento (plea bargain) of 2 years 10 months** for continued corruption in October 2014, plus confiscation of assets worth €2.6 million, including a villa. [il Giornale](https://www.ilgiornale.it/news/politica/torna-libert-galan-lex-ministro-detenuto-che-ha-pagato-tutto-1348814.html) · [Open, 2024](https://www.open.online/2024/07/03/veneto-ex-governatore-galan-vitalizio-condanna-tangenti-mose/)

**Convicted — Renato Chisso**, former Veneto regional infrastructure councillor, accepted a patteggiamento of **2 years 6 months**; the Corte di Cassazione rejected his appeal and made the sentence **final on 24 July 2015**. [PrimoWeb](https://primoweb.it/scandalo-mose-definitiva-la-sentenza-di-condanna-per-lex-assessore-regionale-chisso/) · [Il Fatto Quotidiano, 2024](https://www.ilfattoquotidiano.it/2024/04/30/patteggio-una-pena-a-due-anni-e-mezzo-per-lo-scandalo-mose-il-forzista-renato-chisso-ottiene-la-restituzione-di-parte-del-vitalizio/7531697/)

**Resigned, not convicted — Giorgio Orsoni**, then-Mayor of Venice. Orsoni was arrested in June 2014 on suspicion of illegal campaign financing; his own party withdrew support and he **resigned as mayor** shortly afterward — a political act carrying no legal finding. On the criminal side, a Venice court **acquitted him outright ("il fatto non sussiste")** on one charge in September 2017, while his remaining illegal-financing charge was ruled **time-barred (prescritto)** at both first instance and on appeal; the **Corte di Cassazione confirmed the prescrizione finding on 23 October 2020**, closing the case without any determination of guilt. Orsoni was never convicted. [askanews.it](https://www.askanews.it/cronaca/2017/09/15/mose-assolto-lex-sindaco-di-venezia-orsoni-larresto-fu-un-abuso-top10_20170915_082830/) · [Il Fatto Quotidiano, 2020](https://www.ilfattoquotidiano.it/2020/10/23/processo-mose-prescrizione-per-lex-sindaco-di-venezia-giorgio-orsoni-e-per-limprenditore-erasmo-cinque/5977125/)

**Not counted as convicted:** **Giovanni Mazzacurati**, the CVN president at the center of the scheme, was arrested in 2013 but became the chief cooperating witness against other defendants; no specific final criminal sentence for him was ever recorded, and he was later ruled unfit to stand trial owing to severe illness before his death in September 2019 (he did face a separate, non-criminal Corte dei Conti damages claim of roughly €21–22 million). **Altero Matteoli**, former national Infrastructure Minister, was convicted at first instance in September 2017 (4 years) but **died in February 2018 before the appeal concluded**; the Court of Appeal declared the offense extinguished by his death in July 2019 — the conviction never became final and he is not counted here. [ANSA — Mazzacurati death](https://www.ansa.it/veneto/notizie/2019/09/24/mose-morto-mazzacurati-ex-capo-cvn_6acaa33f-c2ed-4106-8e62-3fa72c028474.html) · [Il Gazzettino — Matteoli 4 anni](https://www.ilgazzettino.it/nordest/venezia/sentenza_mose_matteoli_4_anni_condanne_orsoni_assolto-3239414.html)

**Denial quote:** Despite his own guilty plea, Galan publicly minimized his role, saying responsibility for MOSE lay "tutta… di Roma" (entirely with Rome) and dismissing his accusers as "poveretti" (pathetic souls). [Il Fatto Quotidiano](https://www.ilfattoquotidiano.it/2019/11/14/mose-galan-tutta-responsabilita-di-roma-quelli-che-danno-la-colpa-a-me-sono-poveretti-ma-lui-ha-patteggiato-2-anni-per-corruzione/5564455/)

**Aftermath:** Italy's National Anti-Corruption Authority (ANAC) forced the Prefect of Rome to place CVN under external commissioning; a Liquidation Commissioner was appointed in November 2020, effectively winding down the consortium as the state took direct control of finishing the barrier. In a final coda, on **8 May 2025** a Venice court acquitted CVN itself of corporate/administrative liability, finding the illicit conduct belonged to individual member firms, not the consortium entity. [Il Gazzettino — CVN commissioned](https://ilgazzettino.it/NORDEST/VENEZIA/consorzio_venezia_nuova_commissariato_prefetto_roma_anac/notizie/982593.shtml) · [ANSA, 8 May 2025](https://www.ansa.it/sito/notizie/cronaca/2025/05/08/moseconsorzio-venezia-nuova-assolto-su-responsabilita-giuridica_b1acb044-6454-47a9-9d60-a57e1e22d042.html)

### 2.4 Mafia Capitale ("Mondo di Mezzo"), Rome, investigation from 2014

**Institution involved:** Roma Capitale (city government), across administrations of different political colors.

**Mechanism:** A criminal network allegedly led by Massimo Carminati (a former far-right militant) and Salvatore Buzzi (head of the "29 Giugno" cooperative) bribed public officials to rig Rome city contracts covering migrant/refugee reception centers, waste management, street cleaning, and green-space maintenance. Politicians from both center-right and center-left parties were implicated. Arrests began **2 December 2014** (37 pre-trial custody orders); a second wave in June 2015 added more defendants. Prosecutors seized roughly **€204 million** in assets from Carminati at arrest. [ANSA, 2 Dec 2014](https://www.ansa.it/lazio/notizie/2014/12/02/roma-28-arresti-per-mafia.-indagato-anche-alemanno_36fd7abf-6a18-4ed7-a2e6-7423a28062f2.html) · [OCCRP](https://www.occrp.org/en/investigation/major-organized-crime-bust-explodes-no-mob-in-rome-myth) · [The Guardian, 2014](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2014/dec/07/june-29-co-operative-italy-rome)

**The 2019 reclassification — critical legal point.** Prosecutors initially sought a "mafia-type association" classification (article 416-bis of the Italian penal code), and lower courts and an earlier Cassazione review upheld that framing in 2014–2015. But at the **first-instance trial verdict on 20 July 2017**, the Tribunale di Roma **excluded the 416-bis (mafia) charge**, convicting instead on ordinary corruption and simple criminal association (article 416). An appeals court briefly reinstated 416-bis in September 2018, but on **22 October 2019** the **Corte di Cassazione (Sezione VI, sentenza n. 18125)** issued the definitive ruling: it **excluded the mafia-type classification for good**, holding that the network constituted two separate ordinary ("simple") criminal associations operating through systemic corruption, lacking the intimidation-and-omertà method required for mafia-type association under Italian law. The case was remanded only for sentence recalculation. **Carminati and Buzzi's final convictions are for corruption and ordinary criminal association — not for mafia association — and should never be described as "convicted mafia bosses" under Italian law.** [Il Fatto Quotidiano, 20 Jul 2017](https://www.ilfattoquotidiano.it/2017/07/20/mafia-capitale-la-sentenza-carminati-condannato-20-anni-di-carcere-per-buzzi-19-ma-cade-laccusa-di-associazione-mafiosa/3740493/) · [Il Post, 22 Oct 2019](https://www.ilpost.it/2019/10/22/corte-cassazione-associazione-mafiosa-mafia-capitale/) · [Il Fatto Quotidiano, 22 Oct 2019](https://www.ilfattoquotidiano.it/2019/10/22/mondo-di-mezzo-per-la-cassazione-a-roma-non-era-mafia-capitale-annullata-la-sentenza-dappello-per-buzzi-e-carminati/5527252/)

**Convicted — Massimo Carminati.** After remand, the Corte d'Appello di Roma ("appello bis") re-sentenced him to **10 years** on 9 March 2021; the Corte di Cassazione made this **final on 29 September 2022**. **Convicted — Salvatore Buzzi.** Re-sentenced to **12 years 10 months** on the same date, also made **final 29 September 2022**. [Il Post, 9 Mar 2021](https://www.ilpost.it/2021/03/09/processo-appello-bis-mondo-di-mezzo-salvatore-buzzi-condannato-12-anni-massimo-carminati-10-anni/) · [ANSA, 29 Sep 2022](https://www.ansa.it/sito/notizie/cronaca/2022/09/29/mondo-di-mezzo-la-cassazione-condanna-carminati-e-buzzi-_3b46111a-a529-4b70-b35d-5a9291a52ede.html)

**Other final convictions (Rome city officials):** Mirko Coratti, former president of the Assemblea Capitolina (PD), 4 years 6 months, final 22 October 2019; Giordano Tredicine, former city councilor (Forza Italia), 2 years 6 months, final same date; Daniele Ozzimo, former city housing assessore, 2 years 2 months; Massimo Caprari, former councilor, 2 years 4 months; Pierpaolo Pedetti, former councilor (PD), 1 year 9 months. [Il Fatto Quotidiano, 23 Oct 2019](https://www.ilfattoquotidiano.it/2019/10/23/mondo-di-mezzo-condanna-definitiva-per-nove-eseguito-larresto-in-carcere-lex-presidente-dellassemblea-capitolina-mirko-coratti/5528534/)

**Important clarification — Ignazio Marino was NOT part of this case.** The then-Mayor of Rome, Ignazio Marino, resigned in October 2015, but this stemmed from an entirely separate controversy (the "scontrini" affair, over personal use of a mayoral expense card) — he was never investigated or charged in Mafia Capitale, and he was **definitively acquitted** of the scontrini-related charges by the Corte di Cassazione on 9 April 2019 ("il fatto non sussiste"). He is not named here as connected to Mafia Capitale and is not counted as convicted of anything. [Euronews, 2015](https://www.euronews.com/2015/10/08/rome-mayor-resigns-over-lavish-restaurant-expenses) · [Il Sole 24 Ore, 2019](https://www.ilsole24ore.com/art/il-fatto-non-sussiste-ignazio-marino-assolto-cassazione-ABJ2DlmB)

**Denial quote — Salvatore Buzzi**, wiretapped before his arrest: *"Tu c'hai idea quanto ce guadagno sugli immigrati? Il traffico di droga rende meno."* ("Do you have any idea how much I earn on immigrants? Drug trafficking pays less.") [Il Fatto Quotidiano, 2 Dec 2014](https://www.ilfattoquotidiano.it/2014/12/02/mafia-capitale-buzzi-immigrati-si-fanno-soldi-droga/1245847/)

**Aftermath:** Rome's city government introduced anti-corruption procurement reforms ending non-competitive direct contract awards and mandating commission rotation and transparency publication. Nationally, Italy's 2016 public procurement code (Legislative Decree 50/2016) is widely credited to the combined impact of Mafia Capitale and the Expo 2015 case (below), expanding the oversight powers of ANAC under then-president Raffaele Cantone. [Il Messaggero](https://www.ilmessaggero.it/ROMA/CRONACA/mafia_capitale_appalti_campidoglio_rivoluzione/notizie/1134700.shtml)

### 2.5 Expo 2015 Milan Procurement Corruption, investigation from 2014

**Institution involved:** Expo 2015 S.p.A. and affiliated public contracting bodies for the Milan World's Fair (1 May – 31 October 2015).

**Mechanism:** Dubbed the **"cupola degli appalti"** ("the contracts cartel") by investigators, a bipartisan network of former politicians, public managers, and a construction entrepreneur allegedly divided up public contracts and kickbacks tied to Expo infrastructure, including the Palazzo Italia pavilion and worker-housing "base camp." Arrests began in **May 2014**, roughly a year before the fair opened. [Il Post](https://www.ilpost.it/2014/05/09/inchiesta-tangenti-expo-2015-lombardia/) · [OCCRP](https://www.occrp.org/en/news/italy-universal-exposition-managers-former-politicians-arrested-for-corruption) · [Euronews](https://euronews.com/2015/05/29/expo-exposed-corruption-at-milan-s-global-food-event-leaves-a-sour-taste)

**Convicted via patteggiamento (plea bargain, final under Italian law once accepted and unappealed) — accepted 27 November 2014 by the Gup of the Milan Tribunale:**
- **Gianstefano Frigerio**, former DC/Forza Italia politician — 3 years 4 months.
- **Primo Greganti**, former PCI-linked figure (nicknamed "compagno G") — 3 years, plus €10,000 restitution.
- **Luigi Grillo**, former senator (Forza Italia/PdL) — 2 years 8 months, plus €50,000 restitution.
- **Sergio Cattozzo**, UDC local politician — 3 years 2 months.
- **Enrico Maltauro**, construction entrepreneur — 2 years 10 months.
- **Angelo Paris**, Expo procurement/planning manager — 2 years 6 months 20 days, plus €100,000 restitution to Expo 2015 S.p.A.

Under Italian criminal procedure, a patteggiamento is a negotiated sentence that becomes final and legally binding once accepted and unappealed — it counts as a genuine conviction, though it is a negotiated admission rather than a verdict reached after contested trial, and (given the sentence lengths involved) none of these six served active prison time. [ANSA](https://www.ansa.it/lombardia/notizie/2014/11/27/cupola-appalti-expo-frigerio-e-greganti-patteggiano_31643f39-7c73-4ac1-b653-8a0138c2d77e.html) · [Il Fatto Quotidiano](https://www.ilfattoquotidiano.it/2014/11/27/expo-cupola-patteggia-niente-carcere-per-nuova-tangentopoli/1232751/) · [Il Giorno](https://www.ilgiorno.it/cronaca/processo-expo-patteggiamenti-f604b21f)

**Convicted after full trial — Dario Comini**, former executive of Metropolitana Milanese and director of works for the Expo worker "base camp," accused of accepting €8,000 in cash plus helicopter flights to overlook cost inflation on the camp contract. His corruption conviction of **4 years 6 months** was made final by the Corte di Cassazione (roughly 2021–2022). [Il Giorno](https://www.ilgiorno.it/milano/cronaca/campo-base-di-expo-2015-condannato-ex-dirigente-99379f05) · [Il Messaggero](https://www.ilmessaggero.it/italia/expo_tangenti_campo_base_condannato_ex_manager_metropolitana_milanese-4183119.html)

**Not counted as convicted:** Giuseppe Sala, Expo 2015 CEO and later Mayor of Milan, was convicted in 2019 of a separate, distinct charge (false documentation regarding a tender report, not bribery) and sentenced to 6 months, commuted to a €45,000 fine — but on appeal in October 2020 the charge was ruled **extinguished by statute of limitations**, so this conviction never became final and Sala is not counted as convicted here. [MilanoToday](https://www.milanotoday.it/cronaca/sala-prescritto-piastra-expo.html)

**Aftermath:** PM Matteo Renzi appointed **Raffaele Cantone** president of the newly empowered National Anti-Corruption Authority (ANAC) in March 2014; a special law decree gave ANAC, working with Italy's financial police, direct pre-award oversight of Expo tenders — a "vigilanza collaborativa" (collaborative supervision) model reviewing contracts before signature rather than prosecuting after the fact. ANAC reviewed **153 procurement procedures worth €589 million** and recommended corrections in 109 of them; the model was later made permanent and extended to other public bodies from 2016. [Open Contracting Partnership](https://www.open-contracting.org/2022/09/21/behind-italys-small-revolution-in-the-fight-for-corruption-free-contracts/) · [IMF eLibrary](https://www.elibrary.imf.org/downloadpdf/display/book/9781484339749/ch021.pdf)

### 2.6 Lombardy Healthcare ("Sanità") Corruption — the Maugeri Foundation Case

**Institution involved:** Lombardy regional government; Maugeri Foundation and San Raffaele hospital/rehabilitation network.

**Mechanism:** As President of the Lombardy Region (1995–2013), Roberto Formigoni allegedly used regional government resolutions to favor the Maugeri Foundation (a healthcare and rehabilitation body) and the San Raffaele hospital group, in exchange for cash, free vacations in South America, use of a yacht, and a villa in Sardinia.

**Convicted — Roberto Formigoni.** The Corte d'Appello di Milano sentenced him to 7 years 6 months; the **Corte di Cassazione made the conviction final on 21 February 2019**, reducing the sentence to **5 years 10 months** after charges tied to the separate San Raffaele allegations fell outside the statute of limitations, leaving only the Maugeri Foundation counts as the basis for the final sentence. He was granted house arrest in July 2019 on health-related "collaborazione impossibile" grounds and later moved to community service (affidamento in prova ai servizi sociali) in October 2022, teaching Italian to foreign nuns. [Il Fatto Quotidiano](https://www.ilfattoquotidiano.it/2019/02/21/roberto-formigoni-condanna-a-5-anni-e-10-mesi-in-cassazione-lex-governatore-lombardo-dovra-andare-in-carcere/4988568/) · [Open](https://www.open.online/2019/02/22/che-cose-il-caso-maugeri-per-cui-e-stato-condannato-roberto-formigoni/) · [Il Giorno](https://www.ilgiorno.it/milano/cronaca/roberto-formigoni-servizi-sociali-a76f062a)

**Aftermath:** The case intensified scrutiny of Lombardy's public-private healthcare contracting model and fed into broader national debate on regional healthcare governance and conflict-of-interest rules for regional presidents.

## 6. A Note on Exclusions — People Who Do NOT Appear in This Dossier

Applying the strict inclusion rule (final conviction, or resignation clearly labeled as such) required leaving out several prominent, well-known figures whose cases are widely discussed in Italian public life but who do not meet the bar for inclusion by name as "convicted":

- **Giorgio Orsoni** (former Mayor of Venice) — included above, but only as "resigned, not convicted": his criminal case ended via acquittal on one charge and statute-of-limitations expiry on the other, confirmed by the Corte di Cassazione in October 2020.
- **Giovanni Mazzacurati** (former president, Consorzio Venezia Nuova) — central figure in the MOSE scandal, but he became a cooperating witness and was later ruled unfit to stand trial before his 2019 death; no final criminal sentence exists for him, so he is described by role only.
- **Altero Matteoli** (former Italian Infrastructure Minister) — convicted at first instance in the MOSE case (4 years, September 2017), but died in February 2018 before his appeal concluded; the charge was extinguished by his death in July 2019 and never became final. Described by role only.
- **Antonio Bassolino** (former Mayor of Naples, former President of Campania) — investigated over the Naples waste-management ("rifiuti") crisis, but his criminal case ended in a full, final acquittal ("il fatto non sussiste"), confirmed on appeal; he faced separate, non-criminal financial-liability findings from the Corte dei Conti, which are distinct from a criminal conviction. Not named as convicted.
- **Ignazio Marino** (former Mayor of Rome) — resigned in October 2015, but over an unrelated expense-account controversy, not Mafia Capitale; he was never charged in that case and was definitively acquitted of the expense-related charges in 2019. Not connected to any corruption conviction here.
- **Giuseppe Sala** (Expo 2015 CEO, later Mayor of Milan) — convicted at first instance of a document-falsification charge in 2019, but the charge was ruled time-barred on appeal in 2020 and never became final.
- **Raffaele Lombardo** (former President of Sicily) — investigated for alleged mafia association and electoral corruption, but definitively **acquitted** by the Corte di Cassazione in March 2023. Not named as convicted.
- Numerous other Tangentopoli-era and Berlusconi-era figures whose trials ended in acquittal or were time-barred (prescrizione) are referenced only by role/institution throughout Section 2, per the strict inclusion rule.
