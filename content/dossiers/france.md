# FRANCE — Documented Political Corruption Dossier

*Prepared for Domestic Data Streamers workshop. Compiled July 2026. Legal status verified against 2024–2026 sources. Strict rule applied throughout: only FINAL criminal convictions (all appeals exhausted, including Cour de cassation where relevant) or documented resignations are treated as established fact. Accused, under-investigation, or acquitted persons are named by role only, never asserted as guilty.*

---

## 1. Country Snapshot

- **Transparency International Corruption Perceptions Index (CPI) 2024**: France scored **66/100**, ranked **27th of 180** countries — its lowest-ever score band. Source: [Transparency.org CPI 2024](https://www.transparency.org/en/cpi/2024/index/fra)
- **CPI 2025** (published 10 Feb 2026): France held at **66/100**, ranked **27th of 182**. The report flagged "persistent shortcomings in prevention and enforcement measures" and a "gradual weakening of the anti-corruption regulatory framework." Sources: [Transparency.org CPI 2025](https://www.transparency.org/en/cpi/2025/index/fra), [France24](https://www.france24.com/en/france/20260210-france-sinks-new-low-in-annual-global-corruption-index-democracy)
- **Eurobarometer** (Special Eurobarometer 548, 2024/2025 update): EU-wide, **69%** of Europeans believe corruption is widespread in their own country; **73%** believe there is corruption within national public institutions; **51%** believe corruption is widespread among political parties specifically; only **20%** of citizens who encounter corruption report it. France is among the EU states with the highest share (90%, tied with Spain) saying it is "never acceptable" to pay a bribe to public services. Sources: [Euronews](https://www.euronews.com/my-europe/2025/07/23/do-eu-citizens-believe-that-their-local-and-national-public-institutions-are-corrupt), [eucrim](https://eucrim.eu/news/eurobarometer-citizens-and-businesses-remain-concerned-over-corruption/)
- **Cost of corruption**: RAND-derived estimates, still cited by anti-corruption NGOs and EU-adjacent analyses, put the annual cost of corruption to France at **€120–210 billion/year** (roughly 6–7% of GDP), within an EU-wide estimated cost of **~€990 billion/year** (6.3% of EU GDP). Sources: [Anticor](https://www.anticor.org/la-corruption-coute-120-milliards-deuros-par-an-a-la-france/), [Euractiv](https://www.euractiv.fr/section/l-europe-dans-le-monde/news/corruption-un-cout-de-pres-de-1-000-milliards-deuros-par-an-pour-lue/)
- **GRECO (Council of Europe)**: The Fifth Evaluation Round Second Compliance Report on France (published March 2024) found France had **satisfactorily implemented only 4 of 18 recommendations** on preventing corruption in central government and law enforcement, with 9 "partly implemented" and 5 "not implemented." France adopted a new multi-annual national anti-corruption plan on 16 June 2025. GRECO opened its Sixth Evaluation Round on France (local/regional authorities) in March 2025. Sources: [Council of Europe](https://www.coe.int/en/web/greco/-/france-publication-of-fifth-evaluation-round-second-compliance-report), [Council of Europe evaluations](https://www.coe.int/en/web/greco/evaluations/france)
- **Domestic enforcement**: In 2024, French police/gendarmerie registered **934 proceedings** for probity offenses (+8.2% YoY), of which **324** were corruption-specific. The French Anti-Corruption Agency (AFA) received **802 whistleblower reports in 2024**, up 83–84% YoY, 76% concerning the public sector (mostly local government). Of roughly 18,000 officials required to declare interests, only 5,000 of 13,000 filed 2024 declarations could be verified by the transparency authority HATVP due to capacity limits. Sources: [Agence Française Anticorruption](https://www.agence-francaise-anticorruption.gouv.fr/fr/publication-rapport-dactivite-2024-lagence-francaise-anticorruption), [JSS](https://jss.fr/post/L-Agence_francaise_anticorruption_revele_une_hausse_de_84__des_signalements_en_2024-6047), [OECD 2026 country note](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2026/03/anti-corruption-and-integrity-outlook-2026-country-notes_b1df9263/france_bc0b8603/4f98f194-en.pdf)

## 2. Major Cases

### Case 1 — "Affaire Bismuth" (wiretapping / influence peddling)

**Years:** 2014 (facts) – 2024 (final ruling). **Institution:** Judiciary / presidency.

**Mechanism:** Nicolas Sarkozy and his lawyer Thierry Herzog were found to have attempted to corrupt magistrate Gilbert Azibert by promising him a prestigious post in Monaco in exchange for confidential information about a separate investigation targeting Sarkozy.

**Legal status: FINAL.** Convicted at first instance 1 March 2021; upheld by the Paris Court of Appeal 17 May 2023; the **Cour de cassation rejected the final appeal on 18 December 2024**, making the conviction definitive. Sarkozy's only remaining recourse is the European Court of Human Rights (not a domestic appeal).

**Sentence:** Nicolas Sarkozy — **3 years imprisonment (1 year firm, served under electronic monitoring), 3 years ineligibility**. Thierry Herzog (his lawyer) was convicted in the same proceeding; he is referenced here by role as co-defendant.

**Aftermath:** This is Sarkozy's first final criminal conviction as a former head of state, and the first time a French ex-president wore an electronic monitoring bracelet.

**Sources:** [Lexbase](https://www.lexbase.fr/article-juridique/114070005-breves-nicolas-sarkozy-definitivement-condamne-dans-laffaire-bismuth), [France24](https://www.france24.com/fr/info-en-continu/20241218-nicolas-sarkozy-d%C3%A9finitivement-condamn%C3%A9-%C3%A0-un-an-sous-bracelet-%C3%A9lectronique-dans-l-affaire-des-%C3%A9coutes), [Le Club des Juristes](https://www.leclubdesjuristes.com/justice/affaire-des-ecoutes-la-cour-de-cassation-tranche-sur-le-pourvoi-de-nicolas-sarkozy-8483/), [Cour de cassation](https://www.courdecassation.fr/decision/67626d77d9347f6c9aef808c)

### Case 2 — "Affaire Bygmalion" (2012 campaign financing fraud)

**Years:** 2012 (facts) – November 2025 (final ruling). **Institution:** UMP party / presidential campaign finances.

**Mechanism:** A false-invoicing scheme routed campaign costs through the UMP party and the events firm Bygmalion to conceal that Sarkozy's 2012 re-election campaign spent roughly **€43 million** — nearly double the legal cap of **€22.5 million**.

**Legal status: FINAL as of 26 November 2025** — the Cour de cassation rejected Sarkozy's final appeal.

**Convicted, with sentences:**
- **Nicolas Sarkozy** — final sentence (Paris Court of Appeal, 14 Feb 2024, now definitive): **1 year imprisonment, 6 months suspended**, firm portion served under electronic monitoring.
- **Bastien Millot** (Bygmalion co-founder) — convicted at first instance (Sept 2021) to 3 years (18 months suspended) and a €100,000 fine, for complicity in forgery, fraud, and illegal campaign financing. (First-instance figure; exact post-appeal sentence not independently reconfirmed for this dossier.)
- **Fabienne Liadzé** (former UMP financial director) — convicted at first instance to 3 years (18 months suspended) plus 5 years ineligibility.
- **Éric Cesari** (former UMP general director) — convicted at first instance to 3 years (2 years firm).
- **Jérôme Lavrilleux** (former deputy director of the 2012 campaign) — convicted; publicly stated he would not appeal.

**Aftermath:** This is Sarkozy's second final criminal conviction. His lawyers signaled they may take the case to the European Court of Human Rights.

**Sources:** [Euronews](https://www.euronews.com/2025/11/26/frances-top-court-upholds-ex-president-sarkozys-conviction-for-illegal-campaign-financing), [Courthouse News](https://www.courthousenews.com/frances-highest-court-upholds-ex-president-sarkozys-illegal-campaign-financing-conviction/), [France24](https://www.france24.com/fr/info-en-continu/20240214-affaire-bygmalion-nicolas-sarkozy-condamn%C3%A9-en-appel-%C3%A0-un-an-de-prison-dont-six-mois-avec-sursis), [Ozap](https://www.ozap.com/actu/affaire-bygmalion-bastien-millot-condamne-a-trois-ans-de-prison-dont-18-mois-avec-sursis/608964)

### Case 3 — Libya/Gaddafi Illegal Campaign Financing ("Affaire Sarkozy-Kadhafi") — NOT FINAL, appeal pending

**Years:** 2007 (alleged facts) – appeal ongoing into late 2026. **Institution:** 2007 presidential campaign.

**Mechanism:** Alleged corrupt pact to obtain millions in illegal financing from Muammar Gaddafi's regime for Sarkozy's 2007 campaign, via intermediary Ziad Takieddine, who alleged delivering cash-filled suitcases to Sarkozy and his then-chief-of-staff.

**Legal status — precise, per the strict finality rule: NOT FINAL.** At first instance (25 September 2025), Sarkozy was found guilty of **criminal conspiracy** (association de malfaiteurs) only, sentenced to **5 years imprisonment** — making him the first modern former French president imprisoned; he served **20 days at La Santé prison** before release under judicial supervision in November 2025. At the same first-instance trial he was **acquitted on three other charges**: corruption, illegal campaign financing, and concealment of embezzlement of Libyan public funds. His **appeal trial ran March–May 2026**; on 13 May 2026 prosecutors asked the appeals court to convict him on all four charges (including the three he had been cleared of) and requested a **7-year sentence**. Sarkozy maintained his innocence throughout. **A verdict is expected 30 November 2026** — after this dossier's cutoff. This case must not be described as a final conviction, and the three charges on which he was cleared at first instance must not be described as proven.

**People referenced by role only (not established as guilty):** Éric Woerth (2007 campaign treasurer, acquitted in a related/overlapping matter), Claude Guéant (named by a witness as an alleged recipient of funds in this specific case — not treated as convicted here).

**Sources:** [Plataforma Media](https://www.plataformamedia.com/en/2026/05/14/sarkozy-libya-financing-appeal-trial-seven-year-sentence-2026/), [Courthouse News](https://www.courthousenews.com/frances-sarkozy-denies-corruption-in-libyan-campaign-financing-appeal/), [France24](https://www.france24.com/en/france/20260513-prosecutors-to-announce-sentences-sought-for-sarkozy-in-libya-appeal-trial), [Euronews](https://www.euronews.com/my-europe/2026/05/27/sarkozy-insists-on-innocence-in-last-day-of-appeal-trial-in-libya-case), [Euronews (first-instance verdict)](https://www.euronews.com/my-europe/2025/09/25/former-french-president-sarkozy-found-guilty-of-criminal-conspiracy-in-illegal-financing-c)

### Case 4 — Fictitious Jobs at Paris City Hall (Jacques Chirac)

**Years:** 1990–1995 (facts) – 2011 (final). **Institution:** Paris City Hall / RPR party.

**Mechanism:** Approximately 20 disputed RPR party positions were paid for using Paris municipal funds while Jacques Chirac was mayor of Paris.

**Legal status: FINAL.** Convicted 15 December 2011 by the Paris criminal court of embezzlement of public funds, breach of trust, and illegal conflict of interest. Chirac (then 79) declared he would not appeal, making the ruling final. He remains the first French president convicted by the justice system.

**Sentence:** **2 years imprisonment, wholly suspended.**

**Related, same broader affair:** Alain Juppé, then RPR secretary-general, was separately convicted — first instance 30 January 2004 (18 months suspended + 10 years ineligibility), reduced on appeal 1 December 2004 to **14 months suspended + 1 year ineligibility**, which forced his resignation as Mayor of Bordeaux at the time.

**Sources:** [Wikipedia FR](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Affaire_des_emplois_fictifs_de_la_mairie_de_Paris), [Franceinfo](https://www.franceinfo.fr/politique/jacques-chirac/mort-de-jacques-chirac-de-la-plainte-d-un-contribuable-a-une-condamnation-historique-retour-sur-l-affaire-des-emplois-fictifs-a-la-mairie-de-paris_3634585.html), [Poligraph](https://poligraph.fr/affaires/jacques-chirac-detournement-de-fonds), [Franceinfo (Juppé)](https://www.franceinfo.fr/politique/alain-juppe/alain-juppe-invoque-un-droit-a-l-oubli-sur-sa-condamnation-passee-dans-l-affaire-des-emplois-fictifs-de-la-mairie-de-paris_3201081.html)

### Case 5 — Balkany Affair (Levallois-Perret tax fraud / money laundering)

**Years:** 1980s–2010s (facts) – 30 June 2021 (final). **Institution:** Mayor's office, Levallois-Perret.

**Mechanism:** Undeclared Swiss funds from the 1980s–90s (the underlying tax fraud now time-barred) were laundered through luxury real-estate purchases, including a villa in Marrakech.

**Legal status: FINAL** as of **30 June 2021**, when the Cour de cassation confirmed guilt for aggravated money laundering of tax fraud.

**Convicted, with sentences:**
- **Patrick Balkany** (former Mayor of Levallois-Perret) — appellate sentence (27 May 2020): **5 years imprisonment (firm) + €100,000 fine**, for money laundering and tax fraud. He was **acquitted** on the separate corruption charge (alleging €5.25 million from foreign businessmen for the Marrakech villa) — that count must not be described as a conviction.
- **Isabelle Balkany** (former Deputy Mayor) — appellate sentence: **4 years imprisonment (firm) + €100,000 fine.**

**Sources:** [Wikipedia FR](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Affaire_Balkany), [Smart Legal](https://www.smart-legal.fr/affaire-balkany-condamnation-pour-fraude-fiscale-et-blanchiment/)

### Case 6 — Jérôme Cahuzac Tax Fraud

**Years:** 2012–2013 (scandal) – 15 May 2018 (final). **Institution:** Ministry of the Budget (Hollande government).

**Mechanism:** Cahuzac, the minister in charge of fighting tax fraud, held an undeclared Swiss bank account (later moved to Singapore), and lied about it to the National Assembly on 5 December 2012.

**Legal status: FINAL.** Convicted at first instance 8 December 2016 (3 years + 5 years ineligibility); the appeal court increased the sentence 15 May 2018. No cassation appeal was lodged, making the 2018 appellate ruling definitive.

**Sentence:** **4 years imprisonment (2 years suspended), €300,000 fine, 5 years ineligibility.**

**Aftermath:** Resigned as Budget Minister in March 2013 once the investigation opened, well before conviction.

**Sources:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cahuzac_affair), [Public Sénat](https://www.publicsenat.fr/actualites/non-classe/cahuzac-condamne-a-2-ans-ferme-devrait-echapper-a-la-prison-85951), [Dalloz Actualité](https://www.dalloz-actualite.fr/flash/proces-jerome-cahuzac-sanction-plus-lourde-pas-de-prison-et-pas-de-pourvoi), [Off Investigation](https://www.off-investigation.fr/la-vraie-histoire-du-compte-suisse-de-jerome-cahuzac/)

### Case 7 — François Fillon / "Penelope Gate" (fake parliamentary jobs)

**Years:** 1998–2013 (facts) – 16 February 2026 (final). **Institution:** National Assembly / Revue des Deux Mondes.

**Mechanism:** Fillon's wife Penelope and two of their children were paid substantial sums as parliamentary assistants and consultants for work investigators found no evidence had been carried out.

**Legal status: FINAL as of 16 February 2026.** Convicted at first instance in 2020; the Cour de cassation ruled 24 April 2024 that Fillon was definitively guilty on the merits but annulled the sentence for insufficient reasoning, ordering a new sentencing hearing. The Paris Court of Appeal re-sentenced him on 17 June 2025. The European Court of Human Rights rejected his application on 23 October 2025. Fillon withdrew his final cassation appeal on **16 February 2026**, making the ruling definitive.

**Sentence:** **4 years imprisonment (wholly suspended), €375,000 fine, 5 years ineligibility.**

**Sources:** [France24](https://www.france24.com/fr/info-en-continu/20250617-penelopegate-fran%C3%A7ois-fillon-fix%C3%A9-sur-sa-peine), [Le Club des Juristes](https://www.leclubdesjuristes.com/en-bref/penelopegate-francois-fillon-se-desiste-de-son-pourvoi-en-cassation-14293/), [France 3 Régions](https://france3-regions.franceinfo.fr/pays-de-la-loire/sarthe/le-mans/penelopegate-quelle-peine-pour-francois-fillon-la-cour-d-appel-de-paris-rend-son-jugement-ce-mardi-3172227.html), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fillon_affair)

### Case 8 — Claude Guéant "Cash Bonuses" Affair

**Years:** 2002–2004 (facts) – final (Cour de cassation rejected his appeal). **Institution:** Ministry of the Interior, under Sarkozy.

**Mechanism:** Interior Ministry cabinet members, including Guéant, paid themselves undeclared monthly cash bonuses (€5,000/month for Guéant, on top of salary and allowances) drawn from funds meant for police investigation and surveillance expenses.

**Legal status: FINAL** — the Cour de cassation rejected Guéant's appeal.

**Sentence:** **1 year imprisonment (firm), €75,000 fine, 5-year ban from public office**, for complicity in embezzlement of public funds and receiving stolen goods.

**Sources:** [Public Sénat](https://www.publicsenat.fr/actualites/non-classe/claude-gueant-definitivement-condamne-dans-l-affaire-des-primes-en-liquide-de-l), [France Bleu](https://www.francebleu.fr/infos/politique/primes-en-liquide-claude-gueant-definitivement-condamne-a-un-de-prison-ferme-1547649716), [Franceinfo](https://www.franceinfo.fr/politique/claude-gueant/affaire-gueant/claude-gueant-definitivement-condamne-a-un-an-de-prison-ferme-dans-l-affaire-des-primes-en-liquide-du-ministere-de-l-interieur_3147663.html)

### Political and institutional aftermath, all cases

The accumulation of final convictions against two former presidents (Chirac, and now Sarkozy twice over), a former prime minister (Fillon), and multiple cabinet ministers (Cahuzac, Guéant) has repeatedly triggered institutional responses in France: the creation of the Haute Autorité pour la Transparence de la Vie Publique (HATVP) directly followed the Cahuzac affair in 2013; the Agence Française Anticorruption (AFA) was created by the 2016 Sapin II law partly in response to the accumulation of these scandals; and the sharp rise in ineligibility sentences handed down by French courts since 2016 (see Numbers That Sting, below) reflects a legislative and judicial tightening explicitly linked to this pattern of cases. Each of the final convictions above was accompanied by a period (often years) of the convicted person continuing to hold or seek public office while appeals were pending — a pattern itself criticized by anti-corruption NGOs such as Anticor and Transparency International France as undermining public trust even before any conviction becomes final.

### Notable exclusions (per the strict legal rule)

- **Éric Woerth** — acquitted on both counts (influence trafficking, abuse of weakness) in the Bettencourt affair, 28 May 2015. Referenced only as former Budget Minister / 2007 campaign treasurer; not guilty of anything under this dossier's rule.
- **Christine Lagarde** — the Cour de Justice de la République found her guilty of **negligence only** (not corruption) in the Tapie arbitration affair on 19 December 2016, but **exempted her from any penalty** ("dispense de peine"). This is a real but unusual outcome and must not be described as a corruption conviction.
- **Richard Ferrand** — the Mutuelles de Bretagne case ended in a finding that the facts were **time-barred (prescrits)**, confirmed by the Cour de cassation in 2022. He was never convicted; referenced only by role.

Je me battrai jusqu'au bout,"* denouncing what he called *"un assassinat politique"* ("a political assassination"). — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fillon_affair)

3. **Nicolas Sarkozy**, during the Libya appeal trial, 2026: *"Je suis innocent,"* stating he had "answered tirelessly to all questions, all investigations, all inquiries" for 14 years and expressing disbelief he could face 7 years "for facts he did not commit." — [Euronews](https://www.euronews.com/my-europe/2026/05/27/sarkozy-insists-on-innocence-in-last-day-of-appeal-trial-in-libya-case)

4. **Ziad Takieddine**, key witness/intermediary in the Libya case who later retracted testimony implicating Sarkozy: claimed he had been *"manipulé par des juges qui ne veulent pas connaître la vérité"* ("manipulated by judges who do not want to know the truth"). (Presented as an attributed statement by a contested witness, not a verified fact.) — [Le JDD](https://www.lejdd.fr/Societe/sarkozy-kadhafi-le-temoin-que-takieddine-ne-connait-pas-est-pret-a-temoigner-3909879)

5. **Jérôme Lavrilleux**, on his Bygmalion conviction and decision not to appeal: *"Ça fait sept ans que je vis une espèce de relégation ou d'assignation à résidence... Mon état d'esprit n'est pas de faire appel."* ("It's been seven years I've lived a kind of relegation or house arrest... My state of mind is not to appeal.") — [Dailymotion/press coverage](https://www.dailymotion.com/video/x84jypv)
