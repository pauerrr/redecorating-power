# BULGARIA — Documented Political Corruption Dossier

**Compiled for Domestic Data Streamers workshop use. Research current to July 2, 2026.**

**Legal accuracy rule applied throughout:** this dossier distinguishes strictly between (1) FINAL, irrevocable criminal convictions (court and year stated), (2) US Treasury OFAC Global Magnitsky Act **sanctions** — an executive/administrative designation that is explicitly **not** a criminal conviction and not a court's finding of guilt, (3) resignations following documented scandals, and (4) ongoing/unresolved proceedings (accusations, indictments, trials in progress), which are flagged as such and never described as guilt.

---

## 1. Country Snapshot

- **CPI 2024 (Transparency International):** Bulgaria scored **43/100**, ranked **76th of 180** countries. [Transparency.org](https://www.transparency.org/en/cpi/2024/index/bgr)
- **CPI 2025:** Bulgaria scored **40/100** — its **worst result since 2012** — ranked **84th of 182**, tied with Hungary for **last place among EU member states** (EU average: 62). Transparency International attributes this to "the lack of decisive action against kleptocratic networks, years of superficial anti-corruption efforts, growing attacks on civil society, and the repercussions of the first partially annulled elections in the country's modern democratic history," plus the closure of the Anti-Corruption Commission. Published ~10 February 2026. [BTA/Transparency International](https://www.bta.bg/en/news/bulgaria/1060672-bulgaria-s-score-in-corruption-perceptions-index-2025-is-country-s-worst-since-2)
- **Eurobarometer:** **75%** of Bulgarians believe corruption is widespread in their country (2024/2025 data), versus roughly 70% EU-wide; half of Bulgarian respondents named police as chief perpetrators of bribery/abuse of power. [Euronews](https://www.euronews.com/my-europe/2025/07/23/do-eu-citizens-believe-that-their-local-and-national-public-institutions-are-corrupt)
- **EU Cooperation and Verification Mechanism (CVM):** established 2007 at EU accession to address judicial reform, corruption, and (for Bulgaria) organised crime. The Commission's last substantive CVM report on Bulgaria (2019) concluded "all CVM benchmarks have been satisfactorily fulfilled." The **CVM was formally closed for Bulgaria and Romania on 15 September 2023**; Bulgaria addressed remaining "residual commitments" in June 2023. Post-CVM, Bulgaria is monitored under the EU's general annual Rule of Law Report. [European Commission, IP/23/4456](https://ec.europa.eu/commission/presscorner/detail/pt/ip_23_4456)
- **GRECO:** the Fifth Round Evaluation Report on Bulgaria (19 January 2023) found top officials are **not subject to a proper integrity/ethics framework**, no code of ethics applies, asset-declaration verification is "limited to desk analysis," and no rules govern lobbying transparency. A 2024 GRECO compliance report found Bulgaria had **fully implemented only 7 of 28 recommendations** (11 partly, 10 not implemented). [Council of Europe/GRECO](https://www.coe.int/en/web/greco/-/bulgaria-publication-of-5th-round-evaluation-report); [GRECO Compliance Report 2024](https://rm.coe.int/grecorc5-2024-14-final-eng-compliance-report-bulgaria-public/1680b3f2d1)
- **Track record of convictions:** per Bulgarian Anti-Corruption Fund data cited in EU reporting, of **49 investigations against high-profile former ministers, deputy ministers, MPs, magistrates, mayors and regional governors** monitored over six years, **only 3 resulted in convictions and 14 in acquittals** — a pattern the European Commission's Rule of Law Reports attribute to "procedural impediments" and weak prosecutorial follow-through.
- **Recent institutional development (context, not a conviction):** Bulgaria's dedicated Anti-Corruption Commission was **abolished by Parliament on 28 January 2026**, its functions transferred to the Interior Ministry and National Audit Office, amid concern the Commission had itself become "an instrument of political and financial repression" — a concern sharpened by the July 2025 arrest of Varna Mayor Blagomir Kotsev, an opposition politician who **denies all charges and remains only accused/on trial** (excluded from any conviction claim here) and was released on bail in November 2025. The EU had linked roughly €300–367 million in Recovery and Resilience funding to the credibility of anti-corruption institutions including this body. [Sofia Globe](https://sofiaglobe.com/2026/01/28/bulgarias-parliament-shuts-down-anti-corruption-commission/); [Balkan Insight](https://balkaninsight.com/2025/11/28/opposition-mayor-in-bulgaria-released-after-supporters-crowdfund-bail/bi/)

## 2. Major Cases

### 2.1 Delyan Peevski — US Treasury Magnitsky sanctions (not a conviction)

**Legal status: sanctioned by the US Treasury under the Global Magnitsky Act, 2 June 2021 — explicitly not a criminal conviction and not the finding of any court.** The US Treasury's Office of Foreign Assets Control (OFAC), acting under Executive Order 13818, designated Peevski — described in the Treasury release as "an oligarch who previously served as a Bulgarian MP and media mogul" — stating he "has regularly engaged in corruption, using influence peddling and bribes to protect himself from public scrutiny and exert control over key institutions and sectors in Bulgarian society." Treasury alleged he negotiated with politicians in 2019 for political support/media coverage in exchange for protection from investigations, using an associate, Ilko Zhelyazkov, to run bribery schemes tied to residency documents. The designation blocks his US property interests and bars US persons from transacting with him. This action, alongside sanctions on Bozhkov and Zhelyazkov, was "the largest Global Magnitsky action taken on a single day in the history of the program," covering over 65 individuals/entities. Peevski has filed a US federal lawsuit seeking removal from the list, calling the designation "politically motivated and without merit." As of 2025, a Sofia court ruled he is the sole chairman of the DPS party faction. [US Treasury press release JY0208](https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/jy0208); [RFE/RL](https://www.rferl.org/a/bulgaria-peevski-dps-corruption-sanctions/33072468.html)

### 2.2 Vasil Bozhkov — US Treasury Magnitsky sanctions (not a conviction); charged in absentia, no final conviction in Bulgaria

**Legal status: sanctioned by the US Treasury under the Global Magnitsky Act, 2 June 2021 — not a criminal conviction. In Bulgaria, an accused fugitive, charged in absentia, with no final conviction.** Treasury stated Bozhkov "has bribed government officials on several occasions," citing an investigation (per the Bulgarian Prosecutor's Office) that in February 2018 he paid the then-chair of Bulgaria's State Commission on Gambling 10,000 BGN (~$6,220) daily to revoke competitors' licenses; that official subsequently resigned and was arrested, and the Commission was abolished. Bozhkov, once considered Bulgaria's richest man, fled to Dubai in January 2020 ahead of the sanctions and was **charged in absentia** with roughly 18–20 counts across three cases (including leading an organized crime group, coercion, attempted bribery, tax evasion). **None of these charges has resulted in a final conviction.** He surrendered to Bulgarian authorities at Sofia airport in August 2023 and was arrested on return; as of available reporting he remains a defendant in ongoing proceedings — status: accused, not convicted. He has denied all wrongdoing and accused former PM Boyko Borissov's government of extortion against him. [US Treasury press release JY0208](https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/jy0208); [RFE/RL](https://www.rferl.org/a/bulgaria-bozhkov-detained-exile/32565148.html); [Novinite](https://www.novinite.com/articles/221271/Vasil+Bozhkov+is+returning+to+Bulgaria:+20+Charges+in+absentia+in+3+Cases+await+him)

### 2.3 Tsvetan Vasilev and the Corporate Commercial Bank (KTB) collapse — unresolved, no final conviction

**Legal status: NO final conviction as of mid-2026. Trial in absentia still ongoing after nearly a decade.** KTB, then Bulgaria's fourth/fifth-largest bank, collapsed after a bank run in June 2014; the Bulgarian National Bank seized control. Secondary reporting widely (though not independently re-verified against a primary BNB/IMF document in this research) cites the resulting hole as equivalent to roughly **3.7% of Bulgaria's GDP**. Vasilev, the bank's former majority shareholder, has argued the bank was subject to a "corporate raiding attack" orchestrated by Delyan Peevski with alleged assistance from the Prosecutor's Office and BNB — an allegation, not an adjudicated fact. He was charged with embezzlement "on a particularly large scale" in July 2014, and later with over 100 crimes; a trial of Vasilev and 17 co-defendants began in 2017 (Specialized Criminal Court, later moved to Sofia City Court after the former's closure in 2022). Vasilev fled to Serbia and was granted political asylum there in July 2021; he has been declared internationally wanted at various points. In 2025 his defense unsuccessfully challenged the legality of the judicial panel hearing the case (rejected 5–3 by Bulgaria's Judges' Chamber in June 2025). **The case remains unresolved; it must not be described as a conviction.** [BurgasMedia](https://burgasmedia.com/amp/news/tsvetan-vasilev-deloto-za-ktb-se-gleda-ot-nezakonen-sastav?lang=en); [Novinite](https://www.novinite.com/articles/219771/Bulgarian+Banker+Tzvetan+Vassilev+has+been+declared+Internationally+Wanted+again)

### 2.4 "Apartment-gate" (2019) — resignations following a documented scandal

In March–April 2019, Bulgarian media revealed senior officials — including then-Justice Minister Tsetska Tsacheva, two deputy ministers, and GERB deputy party leader **Tsvetan Tsvetanov** (then widely described as "the second-most powerful man in Bulgaria," and formerly Interior Minister 2009–2013) — had purchased luxury apartments from construction company **Arteks** at prices reported far below market value (in Tsvetanov's case, reportedly for less than a quarter to a third of market value). Arteks had separately benefited from a GERB-backed regulatory change permitting a 34-story Sofia skyscraper. **Tsvetanov resigned from Parliament and his party leadership post on 27 March 2019**; Justice Minister Tsacheva and two deputy ministers also resigned around the same period. Bulgaria's anti-corruption commission subsequently found "no wrongdoing" in a probe into the purchases — **no criminal conviction resulted.** [Balkan Insight](https://balkaninsight.com/2019/03/27/gerb-vice-president-quits-bulgaria-parliament-over-property-scandal/); [OCCRP](https://www.occrp.org/en/daily/9555-bulgaria-more-mps-resign-in-apartments-scandal); [bne IntelliNews](https://www.intellinews.com/bulgaria-s-anti-corruption-body-finds-no-wrongdoing-in-apartmentgate-probe-163315/)

### 2.5 Boyko Borissov "nightstand cash and gold bars" photos (2020) — documented scandal, no conviction

On the morning of **17 June 2020**, low-resolution photographs began circulating in Bulgarian media purporting to show then-Prime Minister **Boyko Borissov** asleep beside a nightstand containing a handgun, stacks of €500 banknotes (some reports describing amounts approaching €1 million), and gold bars, at his residence in Sofia's Boyana district. Borissov disputed the authenticity of some of the photos, claiming an unnamed intruder had staged the scene, and stated: **"I have never owned gold bars or bought such."** The episode intensified a political feud with President Rumen Radev, who denied any role in the leak; opposition leader Hristo Ivanov publicly questioned the images' provenance without asserting definitive proof. The photos surfaced amid a building wave of anti-government protests (see Section 4). Borissov was formally questioned by police about the photos in January 2022. **No criminal charge or conviction resulted from this episode.** [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2020-06-18/a-gun-on-a-nightstand-and-wads-of-cash-escalate-bulgarian-feud); [Balkan Insight](https://balkaninsight.com/2020/06/18/bulgarian-prime-minister-enters-a-survival-mode/); [BNR](https://old-news.bnr.bg/en/post/101582196/police-questions-former-pm-borissov-about-the-photos-of-gold-bars-and-bundles-of-euro-notes-in-his-drawer)

### 2.6 Desislava Ivancheva — rare final conviction

**Legal status: final conviction.** Desislava Ivancheva, then-mayor of Sofia's Mladost district, was arrested in a sting operation in April 2018; prosecutors alleged she solicited a **€500,000 bribe** in exchange for four construction permits. Bulgaria's Specialised Criminal Court found her guilty on **15 April 2019**, sentencing her to 20 years' imprisonment (her deputy, Bilyana Petrova, received 15 years). On appeal, the **Supreme Court of Cassation, ruling 10 November 2022, reduced and finalized her sentence to 6 years' imprisonment**, a fine of 12,000 leva (~€6,100), and an eight-year ban from public office — this ruling was final and unappealable. Co-defendants Bilyana Petrova and Petko Dyulgerov received final sentences of 5 and 4 years respectively in the same ruling. Ivancheva was later released following a presidential pardon, having already served roughly three years pending trial/appeal. This case is repeatedly cited by anti-corruption monitors as one of the very few final, irrevocable convictions of a senior Bulgarian official — an outlier against the broader pattern documented in Section 1. [Sofia Globe, 2019](https://sofiaglobe.com/2019/04/15/former-sofia-district-mayor-handed-20-year-sentence-in-corruption-trial/); [Sofia Globe, 2022](https://sofiaglobe.com/2022/11/10/109747/); [Novinite](https://www.novinite.com/articles/233630/Former+Mladost+Mayor+Desislava+Ivancheva+Pardoned+and+Released+from+Prison)

### 2.7 December 2025 government resignation — resignation following documented scandal

In December 2025, Prime Minister Rosen Zhelyazkov's government resigned (11 December 2025) following the largest wave of social protests since 1989, with drone-footage-based media estimates of 100,000–150,000 protesters in Sofia, driven substantially by public anger over corruption and budget policy — an event fitting the strict inclusion rule as a resignation following an extensively documented scandal. [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2025/12/11/bulgarian-government-resigns-after-mass-protests)

## Key Caveats for This Dossier

- The **KTB/Vasilev case remains legally unresolved** nearly a decade after the bank's collapse — no final verdict exists.
- **Bozhkov and Peevski sanctions are administrative Magnitsky Act designations, not convictions** — this distinction must be preserved wherever this material is reused.
- The **Ivancheva case is the clearest example of a genuine final conviction** identified in this research and should be presented as the rare exception to a broader pattern of near-total impunity for senior officials, per GRECO/EU findings.
- The **KTB "3.7% of GDP" figure** is widely repeated in secondary press but was not independently re-confirmed against a primary BNB/IMF document — flagged for further verification if exact precision is required.
